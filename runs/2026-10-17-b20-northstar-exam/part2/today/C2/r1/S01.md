## Answer

No, Sarah Chen can't approve this herself. The customer is eligible for the 15% discount, but it's above her own approval limit, so Michael Torres (VP Sales) has to approve it.

**What I checked**
1. **The request.** DR-9001 is in the CRM exactly as you gave it: Acme Manufacturing (CRM-2048), NS-500, 15% off a list value of $250,000 ($37,500 discount, $212,500 net). Sarah Chen raised it on 2026-09-23, and its status is "Pending Approval." It's justified as "Contract pricing per Acme Master Supply Agreement," so it relies on special terms rather than standard pricing.
2. **The customer.** The CRM account Acme Manufacturing is the trading name of Acme Mfg. Holdings, the customer named in the Master Supply Agreement. Sarah is the account owner.
3. **The product.** NS-500 is the NS-500 Industrial Controller.
4. **The contract.** The Master Supply Agreement runs from 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31, so it was in force on the request date.
5. **The pricing exception.** EXC-ACME-NS500-15 allows up to 15% off list for Acme Manufacturing, on the NS-500 "only." It runs from 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31, and David Morgan (Chief Revenue Officer) approved it on 2025-03-20. It replaced the older 10% exception (EXC-ACME-NS500-10) for requests from 2025-04-01. 15% is exactly at the limit, so the customer is eligible.
6. **The policy.** Northstar Pricing Policy 2026 applies. It covers requests dated in 2026; the 2027 policy only starts on 2027-01-01.
7. **Sarah's authority.** She is an Enterprise Account Executive, and that role can approve discounts up to and including 10%. 15% is over that. The exception itself says it "establishes commercial eligibility only" and "does not grant approval authority to any Northstar employee."
8. **Who must approve.** Discounts above 10% and up to 20% need VP Sales approval. Michael Torres (EMP-200) is VP Sales, and he is also Sarah's manager.

**Something to look into:** last year's identical request, DR-8104 (15% on the NS-500, dated 2025-09-16), was approved by Sarah herself. I didn't check that year's approval limits, so I can't say whether that was allowed. It doesn't hold up this decision, but it shouldn't be treated as a precedent, and it may be worth an audit.
