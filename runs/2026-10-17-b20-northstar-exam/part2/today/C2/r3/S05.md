1. **The request:** The CRM record matches what you sent: Acme (account CRM-2048), NS-500, 15%, list price $250,000, discount $37,500, net $212,500. Sarah Chen requested it on 2026-09-23 and it is Pending Approval. The stated reason is "Contract pricing per Acme Master Supply Agreement," so it relies on special contract terms, not standard pricing.
2. **The customer:** Acme Manufacturing (CRM-2048) is the same company as Acme Mfg. Holdings (ERP C-1001). The guarantee document says one does business as the other.
3. **The product:** NS-500 is the NS-500 Industrial Controller (PROD-001), list price $250,000.
4. **The contract:** The agreement appears only as a name in the CRM justification. The knowledge base has no text, dates or pricing for it. The only contract reference found is ACME-MFG-2025, and only through its Schedule C, which covers support and service levels, not pricing. So I can't confirm an active contract on 2026-09-23. Sarah's own note says only "I think their agreement covers it."
5. **Pricing exception:** None found for Acme or NS-500.
6. **Policy in force:** Northstar Pricing Policy 2026 (effective 2026-01-01 to 2026-12-31). It fully replaces the 2025 policy.
7. **Sarah's authority:** As an Enterprise Account Executive, she can approve discounts up to 10% under the 2026 policy, so 15% is above her limit. Last year's 15% (DR-8104, September 2025, which she approved herself) fell under the 2025 policy's 15% limit and is not a precedent now.
8. **Required approver:** Discounts over 10% and up to 20% need VP Sales approval, and the approval must be recorded. Michael Torres is VP Sales and Sarah's manager.

**Next step:** Ask for the agreement itself, showing its NS-500 discount term and the dates it covers. If it shows Acme can get 15% on NS-500 on 2026-09-23, the outcome would become APPROVE_WITH_AUTHORIZATION, with Michael Torres approving. The contract alone can't make this an outright approval, because it doesn't change Sarah's 10% limit. If the agreement allows less than 15%, the request should be rejected or escalated for new terms. If it doesn't cover NS-500, the request goes to commercial review.
