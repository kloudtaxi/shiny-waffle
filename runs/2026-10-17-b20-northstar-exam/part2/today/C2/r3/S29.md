## Answer

**No, CR-9204 can't be approved as requested.** The decision is **REJECT_OR_ESCALATE**: $1,300,000 is above the highest limit this customer can have, and a more senior approver can't change that.

**First problem: the request isn't in the ERP.** The ERP has only one pending credit request for C-1001: **CR-9201**. It's also from Sarah Chen, dated 2026-09-23, from $250,000 to **$400,000**, and justified by a third-plant ramp-up, not the guarantee. Nothing called CR-9204 exists. I decided on the request as you gave it, not on CR-9201, and made confirming it in the ERP a blocking condition.

**How I reached the decision:**
1. **Customer:** ERP C-1001 is "Acme Mfg. Holdings", which trades as "Acme Manufacturing" (CRM-2048, Strategic tier). The account owner is Sarah Chen, an Enterprise Account Executive.
2. **Policy:** Northstar Credit Policy 2026 applies (in effect 2026-01-01 to 2026-12-31). Its maximum limit for Strategic accounts is **$750,000**. A parent-company guarantee in force raises that maximum by the guaranteed amount, for the customer the guarantee names.
3. **The guarantee:** Acme Group Holdings, Inc. guarantees what its subsidiary Acme Mfg. Holdings, trading as Acme Manufacturing, owes Northstar. It runs from 2026-01-01 to 2027-12-31, so it was in force on the request date and names this customer. Its liability is capped at **$400,000**.
4. **Maximum limit:** $750,000 + $400,000 = **$1,150,000**. The request is **$150,000 over** that.
5. **Payment history and approvers:** I didn't check the invoices or work out the 2026 approval bands. Neither can change the result: clean payment history can't raise the maximum, and the procedure says a higher approver doesn't fix an over-limit request.

To go ahead, the request would need to be confirmed in the ERP and brought down to $1,150,000 or less, or backed by a larger guarantee. A revised request would need a full check of payment history and approvers.
