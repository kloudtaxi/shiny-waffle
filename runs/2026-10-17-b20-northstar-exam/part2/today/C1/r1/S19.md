## Answer

Probably yes, but not yet. A 6% discount is within Sarah Chen's own approval limit. However, request DR-9111 isn't in the system of record, so no one should act on this approval until someone confirms the request exists.

**What I found:**

1. **The request.** DR-9111 doesn't appear in the CRM discount request log (`discount_requests.csv`). The only Acme request pending on 2026-09-23 is DR-9001, a 15% discount on the NS-500 controller. That's a different request, so I haven't used it. I decided on DR-9111 as you gave it, and confirming it exists is a blocking condition. Its stated reason is "Competitive", which means standard pricing, not contract terms.
2. **The customer.** CRM-2048 is Acme Manufacturing (Milwaukee), and Sarah Chen is the account owner. In the ERP the same customer is named Acme Mfg. Holdings (C-1001). It is not Acme Industrial Supply Co. (CRM-2091), which is a separate company.
3. **The product.** NS-CLOUD is the NS-Cloud Operations Suite. The $120,000 list value matches earlier NS-CLOUD requests.
4. **Contract and pricing exception.** Acme has a 2025 agreement (ACME-MFG-2025). Its support schedule covers NS-500 and NS-EDGE, and says NS-Cloud is not a covered product. No pricing exception for NS-CLOUD turned up. Since the request uses standard pricing, this doesn't matter here.
5. **The policy.** The Northstar Pricing Policy 2026 applies to requests dated in 2026. Under it, Enterprise Account Executives can approve discounts up to and including 10%. The 2026 Approval Authority Matrix sets the same limit.
6. **Her authority.** Sarah Chen is an Enterprise Account Executive (employee EMP-101). 6% is under her 10% limit, so she can approve it herself. The rule that approvals above 10% must be recorded doesn't apply.

**Decision: approve**, but only once someone confirms in Northstar CRM that DR-9111 exists and matches these details.
