Yes, but Sarah Chen can't approve it herself. Acme qualifies for $400,000. Because the new limit is above $100,000, the 2026 policy requires the Finance Manager, Priya Shah, to approve it.

1. **The request.** CR-9201 in Northstar ERP matches what you gave: customer C-1001, $250,000 to $400,000, submitted by Sarah Chen on 2026-09-23, status "Pending Approval". It relies on Acme's own standing. Sarah's email to Priya says the parent guarantee could cover more headroom "later", so this request doesn't depend on it.
2. **The customer.** ERP C-1001 "Acme Mfg. Holdings" is the same company as CRM-2048 "Acme Manufacturing". Both records have the same DUNS number (04-812-7730) and address (2200 N Industrial Pkwy, Milwaukee). The CRM tier is **Strategic**. This is a different company from Acme Industrial Supply Co. (C-1044 / CRM-2091).
3. **The policy.** Northstar Credit Policy 2026, in effect from 2026-01-01 to 2026-12-31.
4. **Payment history.** The rule is that no invoice due in the 12 months before the request may be paid, or left unpaid, more than 30 days past its due date. Two invoices were due in that window, and both were paid early:
   - INV-60412 was due 2025-11-28 and paid 2025-11-24.
   - INV-62011 was due 2026-07-25 and paid 2026-07-22.
   - INV-62305 ($720,000) wasn't due until 2026-10-02, after the request date, so it doesn't count.

   Acme passes.
5. **The maximum.** The limit for Strategic accounts is $750,000, so $400,000 is within it. The parent guarantee (GRT-ACME-2026: $400,000, covering Acme Mfg. Holdings, 2026-01-01 to 2027-12-31) is in force, but it isn't needed.
6. **Sarah's authority.** She is an Enterprise Account Executive, which has no credit-approval authority under the policy. Even the Accounts Receivable Specialist level stops at $100,000.
7. **Who must approve.** A limit above $100,000 and up to $500,000 needs the Finance Manager, Priya Shah. VP Sales concurrence is only needed above $500,000, so none is required here. Priya didn't submit the request, so the self-approval rule doesn't come into play.

Two things worth noting, though neither blocks the decision:
- As of today (2026-10-18), invoice INV-62305 is 16 days past due and unpaid. That's still within the 30-day rule, but it's worth checking before the new limit is acted on.
- That one open invoice ($720,000) is already larger than both the current and the requested limit.
