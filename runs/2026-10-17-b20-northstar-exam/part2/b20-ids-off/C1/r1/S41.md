> Ticket SR-39644 (Acme Manufacturing, NS-Edge vibration data lost for six presses, reported P1): what is its true severity, was a service level breached, what does Northstar owe, who must be engaged or notified and by when, and what must the customer do?

## Answer

SR-39644 is **Severity 2, not Severity 1**, and **no service level was breached**. Both targets were met, so Northstar owes no credit and has no engagement, notification, claim decision or root-cause duty on this ticket. The restoration was met with no time to spare, at exactly 12 hours.

**1. Customer.** The ticket's account is CRM-2048 (Acme Manufacturing, DUNS 04-812-7730). That DUNS matches Schedule C, which covers Acme Mfg. Holdings, Milwaukee. This is not Acme Industrial Supply Co. (CRM-2091), which isn't covered.

**2. Scope: covered.** Schedule C gives a Platinum plan covering NS-500 and NS-EDGE, with coverage from 2025-04-01 00:00 CT. The ticket is for NS-EDGE and was opened 2026-07-23, so it is in scope.

**3. True severity: 2.** The reported P1 doesn't count. Severity 1 needs production to be stopped with no workaround, but the ticket says "the presses are still running." Severity 2(b) fits: condition monitoring was unavailable for presses 1–6, even though it was covered by manual checks every two hours.

**4. Schedule version and targets.** The ticket was opened Thursday 2026-07-23 at 13:10 CDT (−05:00), so Schedule v2.0 applies (tickets opened from 2026-04-01). Severity 2 on Platinum has a 2-hour response target and a 12-hour restoration target, both on a 24×7 clock.

**5. Clock, pause and exclusions.**
- Both clocks started at 13:10 when the portal ticket was received.
- **Pause:** at 15:00 the engineer asked for VPN credentials and set the status to Awaiting Customer. At 2026-07-24 08:30 Johnny Johnson recorded that Acme had provided them. Both ends are recorded, so the restoration clock was paused for 17 h 30 min.
- **Exclusions:** neither applies. There is no maintenance notice for this account (the only one, MN-2607, is for BlueRiver), and the customer-caused exclusion was not asserted.

**6. Measurement.**
- **Response: met.** The engineer's ticket comment at 15:00 asked for access, which counts as a response. That is 110 minutes against a 120-minute target.
- **Restoration: met, exactly at the target.** The clock ran 110 minutes before the pause and 610 minutes after it (08:30 to 18:40), for 720 minutes in total. The schedule says an event exactly at the target meets it.
- **The pause decides this outcome.** Without it, restoration took about 29 h 30 min, which would be a band-2 miss.
- **One thing to check:** the 15:00 status entry also says "Kelly Ballard (Acme IT Architecture) provided them." If the credentials actually arrived before the 08:30 entry, the pause should be shorter and this ticket would be a breach. As recorded, the result is met.

**7. Credits.** None are owed because nothing was missed. No credit claim for SR-39644 is on record either.

**8. Obligations.**
- **Northstar:** none.
  - The Severity 2 escalations only apply when a target is missed: notifying the Support Escalation Manager for a missed response, or the Director of Customer Support and the account owner (Sarah Chen) for a missed restoration. Neither was triggered.
  - With no claim and no customer-caused exclusion asserted, there is no claim decision, credit memo or root-cause finding due.
- **Customer:**
  - Telephoning the hotline is only required for Severity 1, so it doesn't apply.
  - Acme met its duty to provide the information and access Northstar needed (the VPN credentials, recorded at 08:30 on 07-24).
  - There is nothing to claim. The claim window would have closed on 2026-08-23 (30 days after the 2026-07-24 restoration date).
