## Answer

Yes. Under the policy in force, Sarah Chen can approve a 6% discount herself. But DR-9111 isn't in the discount request records, so that has to be confirmed before anyone acts on the approval.

1. **The request doesn't appear in the records.** DR-9111 isn't in the discount request records (`discount_requests.csv`), and there's no CRM entry for that ID. The only pending Acme request dated 2026-09-23 is a different one: DR-9001, for 15% on NS-500 under "Contract pricing per Acme Master Supply Agreement." I decided on DR-9111 as you gave it and didn't swap in DR-9001. Confirming DR-9111 exists is a blocking condition.
2. **The basis is standard pricing.** The justification is "Competitive," so the request doesn't rely on a contract term or pricing exception. Acme's preferential pricing applies to NS-500, and an exception only covers the product it names. Its support Schedule C also says NS-Cloud is not a covered product. So no special terms come into it.
3. **The customer.** CRM-2048 is Acme Manufacturing. In the finance system (ERP) the same company is Acme Mfg. Holdings (C-1001), with the same DUNS number, 04-812-7730.
4. **The product.** NS-CLOUD is the NS-Cloud Operations Suite, list price $120,000 in `products.csv`. The submitted figures match: 6% = $7,200 off, net $112,800.
5. **The policy.** Northstar Pricing Policy 2026 is effective 2026-01-01 to 2026-12-31, so it covers 2026-09-23. Enterprise Account Executives may approve discounts up to and including 10%. The 2027 policy cuts that to 5%, but it doesn't apply to this date.
6. **Her authority.** Sarah Chen (EMP-101) is an Enterprise Account Executive, so 6% is within her 10% limit. No one else needs to approve. In the records, Account Executives routinely approve their own discounts of 10% or less.
