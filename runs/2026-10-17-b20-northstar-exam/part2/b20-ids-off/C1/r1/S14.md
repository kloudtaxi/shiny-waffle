## Answer

Yes. If DR-9106 is genuine, Sarah Chen can approve the 8% herself, because it is within her own limit. But there's one blocker: **DR-9106 is not in the CRM discount-request records** (`discount_requests.csv`). That needs to be confirmed before anyone acts on this approval.

**The request.** I decided on DR-9106 exactly as you gave it. Its justification is "Competitive", so it uses standard pricing and doesn't depend on any contract or pricing exception. The CRM records do hold a different Acme request from the same day: DR-9001, a 15% discount on the NS-500 citing the Acme Master Supply Agreement, still pending. I did not use that record in place of this one.

**The customer.** CRM account CRM-2048 is Acme Manufacturing, whose ERP customer is Acme Mfg. Holdings (C-1001). It is a different company from Acme Industrial (CRM-2091), which also buys NS-Edge.

**The product.** NS-EDGE is the NS-Edge Monitoring Package, with a list price of $85,000. The request's figures agree with that: 8% gives a $6,800 discount and a net of $78,200.

**Contract and exception.** Because the request uses standard pricing, no contract or exception has to back it. Acme's only pricing exceptions I found are for the NS-500, so none would cover NS-Edge anyway.

**Policy.** The Northstar Pricing Policy 2026 applies to requests dated 2026-01-01 through 2026-12-31. Under it and the 2026 Approval Authority Matrix, an Enterprise Account Executive can approve up to and including 10%. Above 10% and up to 20% needs the VP Sales, and above 20% needs the Chief Revenue Officer.

**Her authority.** Sarah Chen (EMP-101) is listed as an Enterprise Account Executive. 8% is within her 10% limit, so no one else needs to sign off. One caveat: her job title in the employee list has no date attached. I found nothing suggesting she held a different role on 2026-09-23.
