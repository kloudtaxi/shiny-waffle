## Question (verbatim)

> Could Sarah Chen have approved a 15% NS-500 discount for Acme Manufacturing on 2025-03-31? The request, as recorded in Northstar CRM: {"request_id": "DR-9101", "account_id": "CRM-2048", "product_sku": "NS-500", "requested_discount_pct": "15.0", "list_value_usd": "250000", "discount_value_usd": "37500", "net_value_usd": "212500", "requested_by": "sarah.chen@northstar.example", "request_date": "2025-03-31", "justification": "Contract pricing per Acme Master Supply Agreement", "status": "Pending Approval"}

## Answer

**No. Sarah Chen could not have approved DR-9101 on 2025-03-31 on the basis it cites.** She had the authority for 15%, but the Acme Master Supply Agreement it relies on didn't take effect until the next day.

**1. The request.** DR-9101: 15% off NS-500, list $250,000, discount $37,500, net $212,500. Sarah Chen raised it on 2025-03-31, citing "Contract pricing per Acme Master Supply Agreement". So it relies on special terms, not standard pricing. I couldn't find DR-9101 in the knowledge base (`CRM / DR-9101` returns not_known), so the details above come only from the record you supplied.

**2. The customer.** CRM-2048 "Acme Manufacturing" is the same customer as ERP C-1001 "Acme Mfg. Holdings" (DUNS 04-812-7730). It is a different company from Acme Industrial Supply Co. (C-1044 / CRM-2091). Sarah Chen is the account owner.

**3. The product.** NS-500, the NS-500 Industrial Controller.

**4. The contract.** The Master Supply Agreement (ACME-MFG-2025) does allow up to 15% on NS-500, but it runs from **2025-04-01** to 2028-03-31. It wasn't in force on 2025-03-31. It also says each discount "remains subject to approval under Northstar's internal policies".

**5. Pricing exceptions.**
- The 15% exception (EXC-ACME-NS500-15, Schedule B to the agreement) is also effective only from **2025-04-01**. It states it "establishes commercial eligibility only" and gives no employee approval authority.
- The exception that was in force on 2025-03-31 is the older EXC-ACME-NS500-10, the "Volume Pricing Exception" under ACME-SA-2023. The 15% exception replaces it only for requests dated on or after 2025-04-01. The knowledge base doesn't hold its maximum discount or its dates. Its name suggests 10%, but I haven't assumed that.

**6–8. Policy and authority.** Northstar Pricing Policy 2025 (in effect 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31) says Enterprise Account Executives "may approve discounts up to and including 15%", and anything above 15% needs VP Sales. Sarah Chen is an Enterprise Account Executive, so **15% was within her own authority**. The 2026 matrix, which cuts that limit to 10%, doesn't apply to this date.

**9. Decision: REVIEW_REQUIRED.** Her authority was never the issue. The problem is that the special terms the request names didn't cover the request date. Those terms don't carry back to an earlier date, and a contract allowing a discount is not enough on its own to approve it. If the 10% exception was capped at 10%, the request is too high for the terms in force that day, and a more senior approver can't fix that. Two things remain open: the DR-9101 record itself, and the terms of EXC-ACME-NS500-10.
