## Answer

I couldn't reach a final decision on SR-39340. NS-Cloud was restored too late, but the record has no time for Northstar's first response, so I can't say whether that target was also missed or what the credit comes to. It is either **$200 or $320**.

**Customer and scope.** The ticket's CRM account CRM-2063 belongs to Cedar Health Systems, DUNS 02-906-4418. The same DUNS is on ERP customer C-1003, Cedar Health Systems Inc. Its Silver support terms (SUP-CHS-2025) cover NS-CLOUD, and coverage started on 2025-12-01, before the ticket was opened. The ticket is in scope.

**True severity: Severity 1, not the reported P3.** The severity guide puts NS-Cloud being unavailable to all of a customer's users, with no workaround, at Severity 1. The record shows exactly that: all users at all 14 facilities had no equipment status or alarms, and no workaround existed. The ticket log also shows it was reclassified as Severity 1.

**Schedule and clock.** The ticket opened at 2026-06-10 07:05 Eastern, which is 06:05 Central, so schedule v2.0 applies (tickets opened from 2026-04-01). Severity 1 runs on a 24×7 clock with two targets: first response within 30 minutes and restoration within 4 hours. I found no recorded pauses, no maintenance notice and no claim that Cedar caused the outage.

**Restoration: missed, band 1.** It was restored at 11:35 Central, 5 h 30 m after opening. That is over the 4-hour target but not over twice the target (8 hours).

**First response: can't decide.** The log only says "A Northstar engineer phoned Cedar", with no time. The target was 06:35 Central.

**Credits.**
- Severity 1 credit rates apply only if the customer phoned the Support hotline before restoration. Cedar opened the ticket through the portal, and a call from Northstar to the customer doesn't count. So the lower Severity 2 rates apply.
- The late restoration earns 5% of the $4,000 monthly fee: **$200**.
- A late first response would add 3%, or $120, for **$320** in total.
- Both amounts are under June's cap of 30% ($1,200). The only other Cedar ticket I found nearby (SR-39207) was opened in May, so it doesn't count toward June.
- The claim was timely. It came in writing by email on 2026-06-29 at 10:00 Central, inside the 30-day window that ends 2026-07-10 at 23:59 Central.

**What Northstar owes:**
- **Escalation:** I could only partly read the escalation procedure. The one surviving line requires notifying the Escalation Manager "no later than that moment". My reading is that this means the moment a target is missed, but that is inferred, not confirmed. On that reading, Hannah Lindqvist (Support Escalation Manager) should have been told by 10:05 Central on 2026-06-10, when restoration became late. If the first response was also late, the deadline would be 06:35 Central.
- **Claim decision:** Marcus Adeyemi (Director of Customer Support) must send a written decision by 2026-07-29. I found no record that one was sent; that date has passed.
- **Credit memo:** If the claim is approved, Paul Brennan (Support Billing Manager) must issue the memo the same day.
- **Root cause:** No finding is due, because Northstar never claimed the outage was Cedar's fault.

**What Cedar had to do:**
- Report a Severity 1 by phoning the hotline, (312) 555-0187. Not met: it reported through the portal, which is why the lower credit rates apply.
- Make a timely written claim naming the ticket. Met.
- Keep a technical contact reachable by phone until restoration. Nothing in the record shows this failed.
