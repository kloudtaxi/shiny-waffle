## Answer

I've finished checking the records. **Short answer: Sarah had the authority, but she can't approve this yet, because the contract the request relies on wasn't in force on 2025-03-31.**

**Authority: yes.** The Northstar Pricing Policy 2025 applied to requests from 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31. It lets Enterprise Account Executives "approve discounts up to and including 15%". Sarah Chen (EMP-101) is an Enterprise Account Executive and owns the Acme account. 8% is within her limit, so no higher approver is needed. The 2026 matrix, which caps her role at 10%, doesn't apply to this date.

**Eligibility: not established.** The request's justification is "Contract pricing per Acme Master Supply Agreement".
- **The contract and its exception both start a day late.** The Master Supply Agreement (ACME-MFG-2025) is "effective April 1, 2025", and its 15% NS-500 exception (EXC-ACME-NS500-15, Schedule B) also starts 2025-04-01. On 2025-03-31 neither had started.
- **An older exception may cover the date, but I can't check it.** EXC-ACME-NS500-10, under agreement ACME-SA-2023, is marked "Active". It is only replaced for requests dated on or after 2025-04-01, so it may cover this date. But its maximum discount and effective dates aren't in the knowledge base, and the request doesn't cite it. I won't assume it allows 8%.
- Under the SOP, a request that relies on a contract term that can't be established needs the document before anyone decides. A contract allowing a discount is never enough on its own to approve it.

**DR-9110 isn't in the system of record.** The knowledge base has no CRM record for it, so I decided on the request as you gave it. The figures you gave add up: 8% of $250,000 is $20,000, leaving $230,000. CRM-2048 is Acme Manufacturing, which is ERP C-1001, "Acme Mfg. Holdings".

**What happens next:** if the ACME-SA-2023 / EXC-ACME-NS500-10 terms show at least 8% on NS-500 on 2025-03-31, Sarah can approve it herself (APPROVE). If they don't, it needs commercial review or new terms.
