## Answer

Yes, it looks like it, but the request ID can't be found, so this approval can't be acted on until that's fixed. The request (DR-9110) isn't in the knowledge base: neither the ID lookup nor a name search returns it. So I've decided on the request exactly as you gave it.

**What I checked, for 2025-03-31:**
- **Customer:** CRM account CRM-2048 is Acme Manufacturing. In the finance system (ERP) it's Acme Mfg. Holdings, customer C-1001. Sarah Chen is the account owner.
- **Product:** NS-500 Industrial Controller.
- **The cited contract wasn't in force yet.** The Acme Master Supply Agreement (ACME-MFG-2025) starts April 1, 2025, one day after the request date. Its 15% exception (EXC-ACME-NS500-15) also starts that day. So the term the request cites doesn't cover it.
- **An older agreement does cover the date.** ACME-SA-2023 allows "up to 10% off list price on the NS-500 Industrial Controller (NS-500) for orders placed from 2023-04-01 to 2025-03-31". The request date is its last valid day, and 8% is within the 10% maximum. Its own record also calls it "Active".
- **Policy and authority:** Pricing Policy 2025 (in force for calendar 2025) says "Enterprise Account Executives may approve discounts up to and including 15%". Sarah Chen is an Enterprise Account Executive, so 8% is within her own authority. Only discounts above 15% need VP Sales approval. Her authority comes from that policy, not from the contract.
- **Amounts:** $250,000 list, $20,000 discount (8%), $230,000 net. These add up.

**Decision: APPROVE**, with two blocking conditions:
1. Confirm DR-9110 exists in the CRM.
2. Correct the justification. It cites the Master Supply Agreement, which wasn't in effect on 2025-03-31, so it should cite ACME-SA-2023 instead.

If the order date actually falls on or after April 1, 2025, the 15% exception applies instead, and 8% is still within it and within her authority.
