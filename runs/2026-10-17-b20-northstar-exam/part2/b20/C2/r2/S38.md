## Question (verbatim)

> Ticket SR-39561 (BlueRiver Logistics, NS-Cloud down for all users): what is its true severity, was a service level breached, what does Northstar owe, who must be engaged or notified and by when, and what must the customer do?

## Answer

SR-39561 is **Severity 2**, not the Severity 1 the customer reported, and **no service level was breached**. Northstar owes **no credit**. Its only remaining duty is a written decision declining the customer's credit claim, which was due by 2026-08-13.

**1. Customer and scope.** CRM account CRM-2051 ("BlueRiver Logistics") carries DUNS 07-331-9025. That DUNS also belongs to ERP customer C-1002, Blue River Logistics LLC. The BlueRiver support terms (SUP-BRL-2025, Silver plan, $2,500 a month) cover NS-Cloud from 2025-01-01. The ticket was opened 2026-07-11 23:20 CT, so it is in scope.

**2. True severity.** The customer phoned it in as P1 and said "NS-Cloud has been down for all our users". But the impact recorded on the ticket limits the outage to the St. Louis dispatch site and says "The other depots are fine." Severity 1 needs NS-Cloud down for *all* the customer's users, or a dispatch or operations activity that cannot continue. The record states neither. Down for some sites and not others is Severity 2, clause (c). Under the Silver plan that means a business-hours clock: 2 business hours to respond and 12 to restore. The schedule is v2.0, which covers tickets opened on or after 2026-04-01.

One caveat: the knowledge base gives me extracted quotes, not the full ticket text. I've linked "down for all our users" and "the other depots are fine" to this ticket because they come from the same ticket record. If the full text says dispatch at St. Louis could not continue, the ticket would be Severity 1. The breach result would not change; the escalation duties would (see point 3).

**3. Clocks.** Northstar notice MN-2607 announced an NS-Cloud platform upgrade from 2026-07-11 22:00 to 2026-07-12 02:00 CT. It was sent on 2026-06-26, well over 5 business days ahead, so it counts as scheduled maintenance. The ticket was opened inside that window. On a business-hours clock, the clocks start at the later of the window's end and the next business hour: **Monday 2026-07-13 08:00 CT**.
- **Response:** an engineer phoned the supervisor at 2026-07-11 23:35, before the clock started. Met.
- **Restoration:** service was restored at 2026-07-12 05:05, also before the clock started. Met.
- **If it were Severity 1:** the clock would start at the window's end, 02:00 on 2026-07-12. Restoration at 05:05 would be 3 h 05 min against a 4-hour target, so still met.

**4. Credit.** The customer emailed a written claim for "the Severity 1 restoration credit" on 2026-07-14 at 08:45. That is inside the 30-day window, but no target was missed, so the credit is **$0** and the claim should be declined.

**5. What Northstar owes**
- **Claim decision:** the Director of Customer Support must send a written decision declining the claim by **2026-08-13** (30 calendar days after receipt). That date has passed, and the record doesn't show whether the decision was sent. The knowledge base doesn't name who holds the Director role.
- **No escalations are owed under Severity 2.** Those duties only start when a response or restoration target elapses, and neither did.
- **If it were Severity 1**, Northstar would have owed:
  - the Support Escalation Manager engaged by 2026-07-12 02:15;
  - Kelly Floyd, the account owner, notified by 02:30;
  - the Director of Customer Support notified by 04:00, because service was not yet restored at that point.

  No notice to the VP of Sales would be owed, since restoration came before 06:00.

**6. What the customer must do.** Nothing more. They met both of their obligations:
- **Phone report:** they reported by phone, which only matters for Severity 1.
- **Credit claim:** their written claim was on time.
