## Answer

Yes. A 6% discount is within Sarah Chen's own authority under the policy in force on 2026-09-23. But there's a catch: **request DR-9111 isn't in the system of record**, so it can't go ahead until someone confirms the request exists.

**What I checked:**
1. **The request.** I couldn't find DR-9111 in the knowledge base. The only Acme request I found from Sarah Chen dated 2026-09-23 is a different one: DR-9001, for 15% on the NS-500 controller, citing contract pricing under the Acme Master Supply Agreement. I did not treat that record as this one. I decided on DR-9111 as you gave it. It cites "Competitive" as the reason, not a contract term or pricing exception, so it is a standard-pricing request.
2. **The customer.** CRM account CRM-2048 is ACME Manufacturing, which the ERP records as Acme Mfg. Holdings (C-1001). The account is Strategic, and Sarah Chen is its account owner.
3. **The product.** SKU NS-CLOUD is the NS-Cloud Operations Suite. Acme's account notes say it is evaluating NS-Cloud and plans a pilot on one line.
4. **Contract and exception.** The request doesn't rely on either. Acme's known special pricing covers only the NS-500 (preferential pricing from the 2025 agreement), and pricing exceptions only apply to the product they name, so none of it applies to NS-CLOUD.
5. **The policy.** Northstar Pricing Policy 2026 is in force from 2026-01-01 to 2026-12-31. The 2027 policy, which cuts account executives' limit to 5%, only applies to requests dated on or after 2027-01-01, so it doesn't apply here.
6. **Authority.** Sarah Chen is an Enterprise Account Executive. Under the 2026 policy, she can approve discounts up to and including 10%. So 6% is within her authority, and no VP Sales approval or recorded approval evidence is needed (those start above 10%).
7. **The numbers add up:** 6% of $120,000 is $7,200, leaving a net of $112,800.
