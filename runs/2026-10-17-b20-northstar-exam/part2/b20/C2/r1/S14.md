## Answer

Yes. Sarah Chen can approve the 8% herself under the policy in force on that date. But the request ID DR-9106 isn't in the knowledge base, so the decision can't be acted on until someone confirms the request exists in Northstar CRM.

**What I checked**
1. **The request:** I couldn't find DR-9106. The only Acme request in the CRM extract is a different one, DR-9001 (NS-500 at 15%, citing the Master Supply Agreement). I didn't use that record. This decision is on DR-9106 as you gave it. Its reason is "Competitive", so it relies on standard pricing, not contract terms.
2. **The customer:** CRM-2048 is Acme Manufacturing. In the ERP it is customer C-1001, "Acme Mfg. Holdings", and the contract says Acme Mfg. Holdings trades as Acme Manufacturing. Sarah Chen is the account owner.
3. **The product:** NS-EDGE is the NS-Edge Monitoring Package (PROD-003), list price $85,000. The request's figures match: 8% of $85,000 is $6,800, leaving $78,200.
4. **The contract:** The Master Supply Agreement (ACME-MFG-2025) runs from 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31, so it was active on 2026-09-23, and it covers NS-Edge. Its only discount term is "up to 15%" on the NS-500 controller, and it says every discount still needs approval under Northstar's internal policies. The request doesn't rely on the contract anyway.
5. **Pricing exception:** The two Acme pricing exceptions are both for NS-500. None covers NS-EDGE, and this request doesn't need one.
6. **The policy:** Northstar Pricing Policy 2026 applies (in effect 2026-01-01 to 2026-12-31).
7. **Sarah's authority:** She is an Enterprise Account Executive (EMP-101). The policy lets that role approve discounts up to and including 10%, so 8% is within her limit.
8. **Approver:** Only discounts above 10% need VP Sales (over 10% up to 20%) or the Chief Revenue Officer (over 20%). Neither is needed here.
