## Answer

**Yes. Sarah Chen could have approved this herself: 8% was within both Acme's contract terms and her own authority on 2025-03-31.** Two things need fixing before anyone acts on it, though. DR-9110 isn't in the knowledge base, and the request cites an agreement that hadn't started yet on that date.

**The request.** DR-9110: 8% off NS-500 for CRM-2048, which is $20,000 off a $250,000 list price. Sarah Chen raised it on 2025-03-31, citing the "Acme Master Supply Agreement". I couldn't find DR-9110 anywhere. The only discount request I found for this account is DR-9001 (15%, dated 2026-09-23). That's a different request, so I didn't use it.

**Customer and product.** CRM-2048 "Acme Manufacturing" is the same customer as ERP C-1001 "Acme Mfg. Holdings" (same DUNS number, 04-812-7730). The product is the NS-500 Industrial Controller.

**Contract terms on 2025-03-31.**
- The Master Supply Agreement (ACME-MFG-2025) runs from 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31, and so does its 15% exception (EXC-ACME-NS500-15). Neither was in force on the request date, so the cited justification is wrong for that day.
- The earlier agreement, ACME-SA-2023, did apply. Its volume pricing exception lets Acme receive "up to 10% off list price on the NS-500 … for orders placed from 2023-04-01 to 2025-03-31." 2025-03-31 is the last day that term covers, and 8% is under 10%.

**Policy and authority.** The Northstar Pricing Policy 2025 (2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31) applies. Under it, "Enterprise Account Executives may approve discounts up to and including 15%", and anything above 15% needs the VP Sales. Sarah Chen is an Enterprise Account Executive, so she didn't need a higher approver. Her authority comes from her role, not from the contract.

**Decision: APPROVE**, on hold until two blocking conditions are cleared: confirm DR-9110 exists in Northstar CRM, and correct its justification to cite ACME-SA-2023.
