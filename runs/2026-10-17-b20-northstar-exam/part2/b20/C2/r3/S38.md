## Answer

# SR-39561: BlueRiver Logistics, NS-Cloud outage

**Result: no service level was breached and no credit is owed.** The ticket is really Severity 1. It was opened during a planned maintenance window that BlueRiver had been told about, so both clocks started when the window ended at 02:00 CT. Northstar met the response and the restoration targets.

**1. Customer.** CRM account CRM-2051 has DUNS 07-331-9025. ERP customer C-1002 (Blue River Logistics LLC) has the same DUNS. They are the same company, and the BlueRiver Logistics Support Terms (SUP-BRL-2025) name that company with that DUNS.

**2. Scope: in.** Those terms cover NS-Cloud on the Silver plan. Coverage started 2025-01-01, and the ticket was opened 2026-07-11, after that.

**3. True severity: Severity 1.** "Down for all users" is not what the ticket records. It says St. Louis dispatch "can't see any shipments," "No workaround," and "The other depots are fine." So NS-Cloud was not down for all users. But a dispatch activity could not continue and there was no workaround, which meets Severity 1 criterion (a). The severity guide takes the first severity the impact meets, so Severity 1 wins over Severity 2's "some, but not all, sites" rule. This reading depends on "can't see any shipments" meaning dispatch had stopped; the ticket doesn't say that in so many words.

**4. Schedule and targets.** The ticket was opened 2026-07-11 at 23:20 CT, so schedule v2.0 applies (tickets opened on or after 2026-04-01). Severity 1 targets are a 30-minute response and 4-hour restoration on the 24×7 clock.

**5. Clock start.** Maintenance notice MN-2607 covered the NS-Cloud platform upgrade from 2026-07-11 22:00 to 2026-07-12 02:00 CT. It was sent 2026-06-26, at least 9 business days before the window, well over the 5 required. A ticket opened inside a qualifying window starts both clocks when the window ends. That is true even if the upgrade caused the outage. So both clocks started at 2026-07-12 02:00 CT. No pauses were recorded.

**6. Measurement**

| Response (30 min) | 07-12 02:30 CT | Engineer phoned the supervisor 07-11 23:35 | Met |
| Restoration (4 h) | 07-12 06:00 CT | Restored 07-12 05:05 (3 h 05 min counted) | Met |

**7. Credits.** The customer's claim treats the full 5 h 45 min outage as breach time, but the window time doesn't count. No credit is owed. The monthly fee is $2,500. The claim arrived on time (2026-07-14, within 30 days of restoration), but there is nothing to pay. I couldn't find the monthly credit cap in the records; it doesn't matter here.

**8. What Northstar owes.** Escalation runs on the same delayed clock:
- **Engage Hannah Lindqvist** (Support Escalation Manager) as incident lead by 07-12 02:15 CT.
- **Notify Kelly Floyd** (account owner in the CRM) by 07-12 02:30 CT.
- **Notify Marcus Adeyemi** (Director of Customer Support) by 07-12 04:00 CT. This applies because the ticket was still open when half the restoration target had passed.
- **No other notices apply.** The VP Sales notice wasn't triggered because service was restored before 06:00. The Chief Revenue Officer notice doesn't apply because BlueRiver is a Standard account, not Strategic. No root-cause finding is due.
- **Decide the claim.** Marcus Adeyemi must send a written decision denying the credit by 2026-08-13. No credit memo is needed.

The record doesn't show whether any of these engagements or notices actually happened, so I can't say they were met.

**What the customer must do.**
- **Phone report for Severity 1: met.** The ticket was opened through the hotline.
- **Keep a technical contact reachable by phone until restoration:** no failed call is recorded.
- **Written credit claim: made on time**, but it earns nothing.

**One gap to check:** the records confirm that notice MN-2607 exists, but not that it went to BlueRiver's designated support contacts. If it didn't, the window doesn't count. Restoration would then take 5 h 45 min against 4 h, a band-1 miss, and a $250 credit (10% of $2,500) would be owed.
