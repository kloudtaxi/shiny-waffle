There's also a blocking problem: **CR-9203 isn't in the ERP's credit-request records.** The only pending Acme request (customer C-1001) from 2026-09-23 is **CR-9201, for $400,000**, with a third-plant justification rather than the guarantee. I've decided CR-9203 as you gave it, but nothing can be acted on until someone confirms it exists.

1. **The request:** Sarah Chen, 2026-09-23, from $250,000 to $1,000,000. It relies on the Acme Group parent guarantee.
2. **The customer:** Acme Mfg. Holdings in the ERP (C-1001) and Acme Manufacturing in the CRM (CRM-2048) are the same company. They share an address and tax ID, and the account plan links them. The CRM tier is **Strategic**.
3. **The policy:** The original 2026 credit policy (CREDIT-POLICY-2026) applies. Revision 2 only covers requests dated from 2026-10-01. I couldn't open the original text directly, so its terms come from facts the knowledge base recorded from it.
4. **Payment history:** No problems. INV-60412 and INV-62011 were both paid before their due dates. INV-62305 ($720,000) is unpaid but wasn't due until 2026-10-02, so it wasn't late on the request date.
5. **Maximum limit:** The Strategic maximum is $750,000. The parent guarantee (GRT-ACME-2026) is from Acme Group Holdings, Inc. It names Acme Mfg. Holdings, doing business as Acme Manufacturing, runs from 2026-01-01 to 2027-12-31, and covers up to $400,000. That makes the maximum **$1,150,000**, so $1,000,000 fits.
6. **Sarah's authority:** She's an Enterprise Account Executive, and the policy gives that role no authority over credit limits.
7. **Required approvers:** Limits over $500,000 need the **Director of Finance (Elena Novak)**. For Strategic accounts, they also need agreement from the **VP Sales (Michael Torres)**. Neither of them submitted the request, so the rule against approving your own request doesn't come into play.
