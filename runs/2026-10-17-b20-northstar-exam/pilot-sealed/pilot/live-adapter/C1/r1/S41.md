## Question (verbatim)

> Ticket SR-39644 (Acme Manufacturing, NS-Edge vibration data lost for six presses, reported P1): what is its true severity, was a service level breached, what does Northstar owe, who must be engaged or notified and by when, and what must the customer do?

## Answer

**SR-39644 is Severity 2, not P1, and no service level was breached.** Northstar owes no credit and no escalation notices. The customer has nothing left to do.

**1. Customer.** The ticket is on CRM account CRM-2048. That account has DUNS 04-812-7730, which matches ERP customer C-1001, Acme Mfg. Holdings, and the customer named in Acme's Schedule C. The account tier is Strategic and the account owner is Sarah Chen.

**2. Scope: covered.** Schedule C gives Acme the Platinum plan and covers NS-Edge (NS-EDGE). Coverage started 2025-04-01 00:00 CT. The ticket was opened 2026-07-23 at 13:10 CDT, after that date.

**3. True severity: Severity 2.** The ticket says vibration monitoring stopped for presses 1–6, the presses kept running, and technicians are doing manual checks. That isn't Severity 1, because production did not stop. It meets Severity 2(b): a condition-monitoring function is unavailable for one or more machines, even if the customer covers it with manual checks. Acme's P1 is only its own view and doesn't set the severity.

**4. Schedule and targets.** The ticket was opened 2026-07-23 13:10 CDT, so Schedule v2.0 applies. All times below are already in CDT, so no conversion was needed. For Severity 2 on the Platinum plan the clock runs 24×7: initial response within 2 hours, restoration within 12 hours.

**5. Clock, pauses and exclusions.**
- Both clocks start at the open time, 13:10.
- The restoration clock paused while the ticket was "Awaiting Customer". The pause began when the engineer asked for VPN credentials (2026-07-23 15:00) and ended when the customer's reply was recorded (2026-07-24 08:30). That is 17 h 30 min, and both ends are recorded in the ticket.
- No maintenance notice exists for CRM-2048, so there is no maintenance exclusion.
- Northstar did not claim the incident was customer-caused.

**6. Measurement.**
- **Response: met.** The engineer's comment asking for VPN credentials at 15:00 counts as the initial response, 1 h 50 min after opening against a 2 h target.
- **Restoration: met, with no time to spare.** Restoration was recorded 2026-07-24 18:40. The total time was 29 h 30 min; minus the 17 h 30 min pause, that is exactly 12 h 00 min. Under the schedule, an event exactly at the target moment meets it.
- **One caveat:** the 15:00 status entry also says "Kelly Ballard (Acme IT Architecture) provided them". If Acme actually supplied the credentials earlier than the recorded 08:30 reply, the pause would be shorter and restoration would be missed. If it isn't shortened at all, the 29 h 30 min is more than twice the 12 h target. The rules say a pause ends when the customer's reply is recorded, and the only recorded reply is at 08:30, so I used that.

**7. Credits: none owed.** Nothing was missed, and there is no credit claim for SR-39644 on record.

**8. Obligations.**
- **Northstar:** no escalation notices were due. The response came before its target. Restoration came at the moment the 12-hour target elapsed, so the ticket was never "not restored" when it elapsed, and no notice to the Director of Customer Support or the account owner was triggered. There is no claim to decide, no credit memo to issue and no root-cause finding due.
- **Acme:** the telephone-report rule applies only to Severity 1, so the portal report is fine. Acme had to provide the access Northstar needed, and did: the VPN credentials, with the reply recorded at 08:30. A claim isn't needed because nothing is owed.
