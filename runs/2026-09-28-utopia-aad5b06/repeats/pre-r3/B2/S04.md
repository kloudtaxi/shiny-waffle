## Answer

I can't confirm it either way, because the knowledge base doesn't record the approval limits by role. What it does record points toward **probably not on her own authority**:

- **Her role:** Sarah Chen is an Enterprise Account Executive. The records put her under Michael Torres, the VP of Sales. That link is stored the other way round (Torres reporting to Chen), but that's almost certainly an extraction error. Nothing gives her role a date, so I can't confirm it held on 2025-09-23.
- **Her part in the Acme exception:** The "Acme NS-500 Strategic Account Exception" was *prepared by* Sarah Chen but *approved by* David Morgan, the Chief Revenue Officer, on 2025-03-20. This pattern (she prepares, a senior executive approves) suggests the Acme NS-500 discounts were above what she could sign off herself.
- **The contract doesn't change who can approve:** Acme's agreement (signed 2025-03-20/22) makes them eligible for NS-500 discounts "up to" a cap. It explicitly states that it "does not modify Northstar's internal approval authority." So being eligible for a discount doesn't let the Account Executive approve it.
- **Timing:** Acme's earlier NS-500 discount eligibility ran from 2023-04-01 to 2025-03-31. The new exception replaces an earlier one, "EXC-ACME-NS500-10", so it likely covered more than 10%, though the records don't say. The recorded NS-500 discount request is dated **2026-09-23**, not 2025-09-23, so check which date you mean.

**What's missing:** The "Approval Authority Matrix — Enterprise Discounts" and the "SOP — Sales Discount Requests" are both in the knowledge base. But none of their percentage limits by role were captured, and neither was the cap on Acme's discount. To answer this for certain, read the Enterprise Account Executive limit in the Authority Matrix document. If it's below 15%, the approval would have needed Torres, Morgan or another senior approver.
