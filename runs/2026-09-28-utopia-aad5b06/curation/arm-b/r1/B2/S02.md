> Can we offer Acme Manufacturing 18% on the NS-500 (DR-9001 at 18%) on 2026-09-23?

## Answer

**No.** On 2026-09-23, Acme could get at most 15% off list on the NS-500, so 18% is 3 points above what they're eligible for.

- **Where the 15% comes from:** Acme (on record as "Acme Mfg. Holdings", with "Acme Manufacturing" as another name) has an NS-500 strategic account exception. It sets their maximum NS-500 discount at 15% of list price from 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31. That window covers 2026-09-23.
- **History:** The exception was prepared by Sarah Chen and approved by David Morgan (Chief Revenue Officer) on 2025-03-20. It replaced an older 10% exception (EXC-ACME-NS500-10), which ran from 2023-04-01 to 2025-03-31.
- **Signing authority doesn't change the cap:** The records also say the Acme arrangement doesn't change Northstar's internal approval authority. The 15% is what Acme is commercially eligible for; who can sign off internally is a separate question.
- **What that means in money:** The NS-500 lists at $250,000. 15% is $37,500 off; 18% would be $45,000 off, which is $7,500 more than Acme is entitled to.
- **No later change on record:** I found no correction or new exception raising the cap.

DR-9001 itself has no details on record beyond its name. The discount request process says to check for an active contract and any pricing exception, then decide who must approve. Here the exception caps Acme at 15%. The approval matrix is on record, but its thresholds aren't, so I can't tell whether anyone could approve 18% on their own authority.

To offer 18%, you'd need a new or amended exception. The current one was approved at CRO level. As of 2026-09-01, the reporting line runs Sarah Chen → Michael Torres (VP Sales) → David Morgan (CRO). Otherwise, the most you can offer today is 15%.
