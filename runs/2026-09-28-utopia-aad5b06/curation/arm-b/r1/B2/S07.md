## Question (verbatim)

> Why is Acme Manufacturing eligible for a 15% discount on the NS-500, and what is the evidence?

## Answer

Acme Manufacturing can get up to 15% off the NS-500 Industrial Controller because a strategic-account pricing exception was approved in March 2025, and the supply agreement signed two days later confirms it. That 15% is a maximum it can qualify for, not a discount it gets automatically. In the knowledge base the customer is listed as **Acme Mfg. Holdings** (customer C-1001). "Acme Manufacturing" and "ACME Manufacturing" are other names for the same account.

**Why it qualifies**
1. **The pricing exception** (`acme_pricing_exception.md`), called the "Acme NS-500 Strategic Account Exception":
   - Sarah Chen prepared it, and David Morgan, Chief Revenue Officer, approved it on 2025-03-20.
   - It applies only to the NS-500 and caps the discount at **15% of list price** from **2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31**. (The start and end dates come from a curation file, `northstar-curation-exception-values.json`, rather than a quoted sentence.)
   - It replaces the earlier exception, EXC-ACME-NS500-10, "for requests dated on or after 2025-04-01."
2. **The Master Supply Agreement** (`acme_master_supply_agreement.md`), signed 2025-03-22 by Edward Floyd, Acme's VP Procurement: *"Customer is eligible for discounts of up to 15% on the NS-500 Industrial Controller, as set out in the pricing exception attached as Schedule B."*
3. **Background** (`acme_account_strategy.md`): "Acme is one of our strategic accounts" and "Acme negotiated preferential NS-500 pricing in the 2025 agreement."

**How the rate changed over time**
- 2023-04-01 to 2025-03-31: up to **10%** under the 2023 volume pricing exception (`acme_pricing_exception_2023.md`).
- 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31: up to **15%**, so 15% applies today (2026-09-28).
- At the NS-500's list price of $250,000, the maximum works out to $37,500 off per unit.

**Limits on the 15%**
- The exception says it "establishes commercial eligibility only." The supply agreement adds that this "does not modify Northstar's internal approval authority. Each discount remains subject to approval under Northstar's internal policies." Each actual discount still needs approval through the normal process.
- Don't give "Acme standardised on the NS-500 across two plants in 2025" as a reason. That claim came from the account strategy document and has been **rejected** in the knowledge base.
- The knowledge base has no automated business rules, so this comes straight from the documents above, not from a system calculation.
