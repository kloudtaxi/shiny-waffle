## Answer

Yes. Going by the knowledge base, Sarah Chen could have approved a 15% NS-500 discount for Acme Manufacturing on 2025-09-23. The one assumption is that she already held her current role then (see below).

**1. Acme was eligible for 15%.** Acme Manufacturing is recorded as "Acme Mfg. Holdings." The Acme NS-500 Strategic Account Exception sets Acme's maximum NS-500 discount at **15% of list price** from 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31. It replaced the earlier 10% cap (EXC-ACME-NS500-10), which covered 2023-04-01 to 2025-03-31. David Morgan, the Chief Revenue Officer, approved the exception on 2025-03-20, and Sarah Chen prepared it. So 15% was the most Acme could get, and it was allowed.

**2. Her role could approve up to and including 15% in 2025.** Northstar Pricing Policy 2025 ran from 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31. It let Enterprise Account Executives approve discounts **up to and including 15%**. Only discounts above 15% needed VP Sales approval. Sarah Chen is listed as an Enterprise Account Executive. A 15% discount sat exactly at her limit, so she could have approved it herself.

**The answer depends on the date.** Northstar Pricing Policy 2026 took effect on 2026-01-01 and lowered the Account Executive limit to **10%**. Under it, anything above 10% and up to 20% needs VP Sales approval from Michael Torres, and anything above 20% needs Chief Revenue Officer approval. The same 15% discount in 2026 would have needed Michael Torres's sign-off. There is also a separate NS-500 discount request dated 2026-09-23, exactly one year after the date you asked about. If you actually meant that one, she could not have approved it alone.

**Assumption to check:** the record of Sarah as an Enterprise Account Executive has no date. Her reporting line to Michael Torres is only recorded from 2026-09-01. I assumed she held the same role in September 2025, but nothing in the knowledge base confirms it for that date.
