**The three identifiers aren't recorded.** Searches for "CRM-2048", "C-1001" and "ACME-MFG-2025", and for pieces of them ("2048", "1001", "MFG-2025"), return nothing. Other IDs in the same formats do exist, such as CRM-2051, CRM-2063, C-1002, C-1003, ACME-SA-2023 and EXC-ACME-NS500-10. So these three were probably never extracted from the source files rather than being invalid. The "ACME-MFG" pattern suggests Acme Manufacturing, but that is only a guess from the name format. I haven't linked them to any customer.

**The Acme manufacturer.** Its canonical name in the graph is **Acme Mfg. Holdings**, and it also appears as "Acme Manufacturing", "ACME Manufacturing" and "Acme". It is:
- a strategic account in Industrial Manufacturing;
- the customer on the NS-500 Industrial Controller exceptions: EXC-ACME-NS500-10 allowed up to 10% (2023-04-01 to 2025-03-31), and the Acme NS-500 Strategic Account Exception allows up to 15% (2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31);
- the customer on sales order SO-7002 (2026-08-18).

**Acme Industrial Supply Co. is a different customer.** It is in Industrial Distribution, not Manufacturing. Its own IDs are **CRM-2091** (CRM, also listed as "Acme Industrial") and **C-1044** (ERP). The graph finds no link between it and Acme Mfg. Holdings within three steps. It doesn't share the Acme manufacturer's NS-500 exceptions: a pricing exception only applies to the customer and product it names.

To confirm the three IDs, check the source files directly: `crm_accounts.csv` for CRM-2048, `customers.csv` for C-1001 and the contract records for ACME-MFG-2025. Or tell me where they appeared, for example a discount request, and I'll trace them from there. Until then, don't assume they're Acme Mfg. Holdings. Above all, don't use them to apply Acme Mfg. Holdings' contract terms or NS-500 exception to Acme Industrial Supply Co.
