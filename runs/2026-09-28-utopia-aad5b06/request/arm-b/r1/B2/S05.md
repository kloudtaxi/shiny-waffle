## Answer

I can't approve DR-9001 yet: **request evidence**. The request relies on the "Acme Master Supply Agreement," but I couldn't find it in the knowledge base, so Acme's contract eligibility for 15% isn't established.

**What I found:**
1. **Request:** DR-9001 asks for 15% off NS-500 ($250,000 list, $37,500 discount, $212,500 net). Sarah Chen requested it on 2026-09-23. The CRM justification is "Contract pricing per Acme Master Supply Agreement," so the basis is a contract term, not standard pricing.
2. **Customer:** CRM account CRM-2048, "Acme Manufacturing" (2200 N Industrial Pkwy, Milwaukee), is the same company as ERP customer C-1001, "Acme Mfg. Holdings." It is not "Acme Industrial Supply Co." (C-1044, Akron), which is a different customer.
3. **Product:** NS-500 Industrial Controller, list price $250,000.
4. **Active contract:** I found no contract entity at all: no Master Supply Agreement and no contract ID or dates for Acme / C-1001. The graph has an entity called "Contract-specific pricing exceptions," but nothing is linked to it.
5. **Pricing exception:** I found no exception covering NS-500 for Acme on 2026-09-23.
6. **Policy:** Northstar Pricing Policy 2026 was in force (2026-01-01 to 2026-12-31). It replaced the 2025 policy, which let account executives approve up to 15%. That no longer applies.
7. **Sarah's authority:** Sarah Chen is an Enterprise Account Executive. Under the 2026 policy she can approve up to and including 10%, so 15% is outside her authority.
8. **Required approver:** Discounts above 10% and up to 20% need VP Sales approval. That's Michael Torres, who is also Sarah's manager (recorded from 2026-09-01).

**Decision:** The contract term the request depends on can't be found, so no one should approve it yet, including Michael Torres. Under the SOP, a customer contract can't be the only reason to approve, and a missing contract term means asking for the document first.

**Next step:** Ask Sarah for the Acme Master Supply Agreement or the relevant pricing exception, covering NS-500 and in effect on 2026-09-23. If it allows at least 15%, the outcome becomes approve with authorization, with Michael Torres signing off. If it caps the discount below 15%, the request should be rejected or escalated for amended terms. If it doesn't cover NS-500 or that date, it goes to commercial review.

One limit on this check: the search tool that reads the documents directly wasn't available, so I could only check the knowledge graph. The agreement may exist as a document the graph doesn't model, which is another reason to ask for it.
