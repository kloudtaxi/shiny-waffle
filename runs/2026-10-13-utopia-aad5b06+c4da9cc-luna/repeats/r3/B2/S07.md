## Question (verbatim)

> Why is Acme Manufacturing eligible for a 15% discount on the NS-500, and what is the evidence?

## Answer

Acme Manufacturing is eligible because of a written exception, **EXC-ACME-NS500-15** (the "Acme NS-500 Strategic Account Exception"). It allows a discount of up to 15% of list price on the NS-500 Industrial Controller.

**What the exception says:**
- **Customer:** Acme Manufacturing (account CRM-2048). Its account tier is Strategic, which fits the exception's name.
- **Product:** NS-500 Industrial Controller, list price $250,000. The knowledge base records the exception as not applying to other Northstar products.
- **Maximum discount:** 15% of list price. That works out to at most $37,500 off, or a floor of about $212,500 per unit.
- **Effective:** 2025-04-01. **Expires:** 2028-03-31, so it is still in force today (2026-10-13).
- **Approved by:** David Morgan, Chief Revenue Officer, on 2025-03-20.
- **Prepared by:** Sarah Chen, Acme's account owner.
- **Replaces:** an earlier exception, **EXC-ACME-NS500-10**. Going by its ID, that one was probably a 10% exception, but the knowledge base doesn't record its rate. A separate fact shows Acme was allowed a discount on the NS-500 from 2023-04-01 to 2025-03-31. The new exception started the day after that ended (2025-04-01).

**Why it matters now:** A record dated 2026-09-23 (a contact named Gina) says Acme wants to go ahead with the NS-500 and is asking for 15% off list. That is exactly the maximum the current exception allows.

**Two limits:**
1. The exception sets out what Acme is commercially eligible for, but it **does not give anyone authority to approve the discount**. The deal still needs normal discount approval. The knowledge base doesn't say who that approver is.
2. 15% is a **ceiling**, not a set price.

I found no corrections or withdrawals on Acme's records, so none of this has been superseded. The knowledge base has no automated rules; all of this comes straight from the exception document and related records.
