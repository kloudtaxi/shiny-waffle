I can't confirm it from the knowledge base, and the evidence suggests she most likely could not have approved it alone. The 2025 pricing policy's exact approval limit for her role isn't recorded, so I can't give a firm yes or no.

- **The policy in force:** Northstar Pricing Policy 2025 covered 2025-01-01 through 2025-12-31. It lets Enterprise Account Executives approve discounts, but some discounts need VP Sales approval. The percentage where her authority ends isn't recorded. One 2026 document says approvals above 10% must be recorded in the CRM, which suggests 10% is a key cut-off, but that is a 2026 rule.
- **The Acme exception doesn't help her:** The Acme NS-500 Strategic Account Exception (EXC-ACME-NS500-15) makes Acme *eligible* for up to 15% off list on the NS-500. It ran from 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31, so it covered 2025-09-23. But it says it **"does not grant approval authority."** David Morgan approved the exception on 2025-03-20, and Sarah Chen prepared it. So Acme could receive 15%, but the exception gives Sarah no power to sign it off.

**One record you should look at:** Request DR-8104 is a 15% NS-500 discount for Acme ($250,000 list, $37,500 off, $212,500 net). It was requested by Sarah and also **approved by Sarah on 2025-09-16**, a week before your date. The stated reason is "Contract pricing per Acme Master Supply Agreement." There is no record of an approval on 2025-09-23 itself.

**Bottom line:** The discount amount was allowed under the exception. Whether she had the authority depends on the 2025 policy's limit for her role, which the knowledge base doesn't hold. If 15% was above that limit, it needed Michael Torres's (VP Sales) approval, and her own approval of DR-8104 would be a policy breach. The next step is to read the limit table in the 2025 pricing policy document.
