## Answer

**Yes, at 8% as you submitted it, Sarah Chen can approve DR-9001 herself. But the CRM record for DR-9001 shows a different discount, so confirm which figure is correct before anything is approved.**

**The figures don't match.** In the knowledge base, DR-9001 is recorded at **15%** ($37,500 off $250,000, net $212,500), not 8%. Sarah's email to Michael Torres on 2026-09-23 also says Acme is "asking for 15% off list, which is what we did for them last September." That matches the earlier request DR-8104 (15%, approved 2025-09-16).
- **If 8% is correct:** the outcome is `APPROVE`. She doesn't need anyone else to sign off.
- **If 15% is correct:** the outcome is `APPROVE_WITH_AUTHORIZATION`. 15% is above her 10% limit, so Michael Torres, as VP Sales, has to approve it and record that approval in CRM. Approving it last year doesn't carry over: under the 2025 policy she could approve up to 15%, but under the 2026 policy her limit is 10%.

**How I reached this**
1. **Customer:** CRM-2048 is Acme Manufacturing, which is called Acme Mfg. Holdings (C-1001) in ERP. Sarah owns the account.
2. **Contract:** The Master Supply Agreement ACME-MFG-2025 was in force on 2026-09-23 (it runs 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31) and covers the NS-500.
3. **Pricing exception:** EXC-ACME-NS500-15 lets Acme get up to 15% off list on the **NS-500 only**, from 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31. It replaces the older 10% exception. 8% is within that limit.
4. **Policy:** The Northstar Pricing Policy 2026 applies. The 2027 policy, which cuts the account executive limit to 5%, doesn't start until 2027-01-01.
5. **Sarah's authority:** As an Enterprise Account Executive she can approve up to 10% under the 2026 policy. Above 10% and up to 20% needs VP Sales; above 20% needs the Chief Revenue Officer.
6. Both the contract (section 3.3) and the exception say they don't give anyone approval authority. That doesn't change the 8% case, because her own role covers it.
