## Answer

**Yes, at 8% as you submitted it, Sarah Chen can approve DR-9001 herself. But the CRM record for DR-9001 shows a different amount, so confirm the real figure before acting.**

**The figures don't match.** The CRM discount requests file holds DR-9001 at **15.0%**: $37,500 off a $250,000 list price, net $212,500. It has the same account, product, requestor, date and justification as your version. Your version says 8.0% ($20,000 off, net $230,000). Which figure is right changes who has to approve it:

- **At 8% (as submitted):** within Sarah's own limit, so she can approve it.
- **At 15% (CRM record):** still within what Acme is entitled to, but above Sarah's 10% limit. The VP Sales, Michael Torres, would have to approve it and record the approval in CRM, because approvals above 10% must be recorded there.

**What was checked:**
1. **Customer:** CRM account CRM-2048 is Acme Manufacturing. The contract names it as Acme Mfg. Holdings, doing business as Acme Manufacturing. Sarah Chen owns the account. It is not the separate account Acme Industrial Supply Co. (CRM-2091).
2. **Product:** NS-500 is the NS-500 Industrial Controller, which the contract covers.
3. **Contract:** The Master Supply Agreement (ACME-MFG-2025) runs from 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31, so it was in force on the request date, 2026-09-23.
4. **Pricing exception:** Exception EXC-ACME-NS500-15 is Schedule B to that agreement. It covers NS-500 only, with a maximum of 15% off list, for the same dates. It replaced the older 10% exception (EXC-ACME-NS500-10). Both 8% and 15% fall within it.
5. **Policy:** The 2026 Pricing Policy and the Approval Authority Matrix apply. Under them, an Enterprise Account Executive can approve up to and including 10%, VP Sales covers above 10% up to 20%, and the Chief Revenue Officer covers anything above 20%. Sarah Chen is an Enterprise Account Executive.
6. **Authority vs. eligibility:** Both the SOP and section 3.3 of the contract say that what the customer is entitled to under the contract does not change who inside the company may approve a discount. So the 15% exception alone does not let Sarah approve 15%.
