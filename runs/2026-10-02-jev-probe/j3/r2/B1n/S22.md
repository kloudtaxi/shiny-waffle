**Yes, at 8% as you submitted it, Sarah can approve DR-9001 herself. But Northstar's CRM (its customer records system) shows DR-9001 at 15%, not 8%.** Please confirm the actual figure before anyone approves it.

**The figures don't match.** Your copy says 8.0%, a $20,000 discount and $230,000 net. The CRM record (discount_requests.csv) has the same ID, account, product, date and justification, but shows 15.0%, $37,500 and $212,500. Sarah's email to Michael Torres the same morning also says Acme is "asking for 15% off list, which is what we did for them last September." The 15% they got last September was DR-8104, approved in 2025, when the 2025 policy let account executives approve up to 15%.

**At 8%, as submitted:**
- **Customer:** CRM account CRM-2048, Acme Manufacturing, is the same customer as ERP account C-1001, Acme Mfg. Holdings. Sarah owns the account.
- **Product:** NS-500 Industrial Controller.
- **Contract:** The Master Supply Agreement (ACME-MFG-2025) runs from 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31, so it was active on the request date, 2026-09-23. Its §3.3 says the contract doesn't change Northstar's internal approval rules.
- **Pricing exception:** EXC-ACME-NS500-15 covers the NS-500 only, allows up to 15% off list, and runs 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31. It replaced the older 10% exception. An 8% discount is within it.
- **Policy:** Under the 2026 pricing policy and the 2026 Approval Authority Matrix, account executives can approve up to 10%. Between 10% and 20% needs the VP Sales, and above 20% needs the Chief Revenue Officer.
- **Sarah's authority:** She is an Enterprise Account Executive, so 8% is within her own limit.

**At 15%, as recorded in CRM:** Acme is still entitled to it, since 15% is the exception's maximum. But it's above Sarah's 10% limit, so the result would be **approve with authorization**: Michael Torres, VP Sales and Sarah's manager, must approve it and the approval must be recorded in CRM. The contract and exception don't give Sarah any extra authority; the exception document says so directly.

The 2027 policy, which lowers the account executive limit to 5%, takes effect on 2027-01-01, so it doesn't apply here.
