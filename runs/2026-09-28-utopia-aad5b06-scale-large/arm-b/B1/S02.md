## Question (verbatim)

> Can we offer Acme Manufacturing 18% on the NS-500 (DR-9001 at 18%) on 2026-09-23?

## Answer

**No, not as things stand.** Two separate problems: 18% is more than Acme's contract allows, and whoever offers it would need VP Sales approval, which the requester doesn't have.

**1. 18% is more than Acme is contractually eligible for.**
- Acme's Master Supply Agreement (ACME-MFG-2025) and its pricing exception (EXC-ACME-NS500-15) are in force on 2026-09-23. Both run from 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31.
- Both cap the NS-500 discount at **15% off list**. So "contract pricing per the Acme Master Supply Agreement," which is how DR-9001 is justified in the CRM, can support at most 15%.
- Also, DR-9001 is actually logged at **15%**, not 18%: $250,000 list, $37,500 discount, $212,500 net, status Pending. Sarah Chen's 2026-09-23 email also says Acme asked for 15%, the same as last September's DR-8104. An 18% offer would therefore be a new request, not DR-9001 as recorded.

**2. Approval authority.** The contract only sets what Acme can get. It doesn't give anyone at Northstar the power to approve it. The exception, section 3.3 of the agreement and the discount procedure (SOP-SALES-014) all say this. Under the 2026 Pricing Policy and Approval Authority Matrix:
- Enterprise Account Executives, such as Sarah Chen, can approve up to 10%.
- Anything over 10% and up to 20% needs **VP Sales** approval. That's Michael Torres.
- Anything over 20% needs the Chief Revenue Officer (David Morgan).

So 18% is within Michael Torres's authority, and the approval would have to be recorded in the CRM. Sarah can't approve it herself, and neither can she approve the 15%.

**What to do:**
- **15%:** Send DR-9001 to Michael Torres for approval and record it in the CRM. The contract covers this.
- **18%:** This needs a separate, non-contract reason (for example, competitive pricing), Michael Torres's approval and a CRM record. The pricing exception was approved by the CRO, so you might also want to check with Deal Desk or David Morgan before going past its 15% cap. I didn't find a policy that explicitly forbids going above it.
