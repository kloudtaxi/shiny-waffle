## Answer

No, Sarah can't approve DR-9001, and it can't go forward as a contract-pricing discount at all. The Acme special terms we have only cover the NS-500 Industrial Controller, not NS-Cloud. The outcome is **REVIEW_REQUIRED**: the request needs commercial review before anyone looks at approval.

1. **Request:** DR-9001 was raised by Sarah Chen on 2026-09-23 for 15% off NS-CLOUD ($120,000 list, $18,000 discount). The stated reason is "Contract pricing per Acme Master Supply Agreement", so it relies on special terms, not standard pricing.
2. **Customer:** In CRM this is Acme Manufacturing, owned by Sarah Chen. The ERP customer is C-1001, "Acme Mfg. Holdings", which the knowledge base treats as the same customer.
3. **Product:** NS-CLOUD is the NS-Cloud Operations Suite.
4. **Contract:** The Master Supply Agreement runs from 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31, so it was active on 2026-09-23. But the only discount term on record for it is up to 15% on the NS-500 Industrial Controller. Nothing links it to NS-Cloud.
5. **Pricing exception:** The Acme NS-500 Strategic Account Exception (maximum 15%, 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31) replaced the earlier 10% exception, EXC-ACME-NS500-10. It "applies only to" the NS-500 and says it doesn't apply to other Northstar products. So there's no exception for NS-Cloud, and the NS-500 terms can't be carried over to it.
6. **Policy:** Northstar Pricing Policy 2026 was in force on the request date.
7. **Sarah's authority:** As an Enterprise Account Executive she can approve up to and including 10%. 15% is over her limit, and customer terms don't change that.
8. **Approver:** Discounts over 10% and up to 20% need VP Sales, which is Michael Torres (Sarah reports to him). If commercial review does set up terms that cover NS-Cloud at 15% or more, the request would still need Michael Torres's approval.

The MSA text isn't in the knowledge base, only its effective dates and the NS-500 term. If Sarah thinks the MSA has an NS-Cloud pricing clause, she should provide that clause for the review.
