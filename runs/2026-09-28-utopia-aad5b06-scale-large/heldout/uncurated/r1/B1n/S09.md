## Question (verbatim)

> Could Sarah Chen have approved a 15% NS-500 discount for Acme Manufacturing on 2025-03-31? The request, as recorded in Northstar CRM: {"request_id": "DR-9101", "account_id": "CRM-2048", "product_sku": "NS-500", "requested_discount_pct": "15.0", "list_value_usd": "250000", "discount_value_usd": "37500", "net_value_usd": "212500", "requested_by": "sarah.chen@northstar.example", "request_date": "2025-03-31", "justification": "Contract pricing per Acme Master Supply Agreement", "status": "Pending Approval"}

## Answer

No. Sarah Chen should not approve DR-9101 as submitted. The outcome is **REJECT_OR_ESCALATE**. Her approval authority isn't the problem; the problem is that the terms the request relies on don't apply until the next day.

1. **The request.** DR-9101 was entered by Sarah Chen on 2025-03-31 for account CRM-2048. It asks for 15% off NS-500, which is $37,500 off a $250,000 list price. The stated justification is "Contract pricing per Acme Master Supply Agreement", so the request relies on contract terms, not standard pricing.
2. **The customer.** CRM-2048 is Acme Manufacturing, which Sarah Chen owns. It is billed in ERP as Acme Mfg. Holdings (C-1001).
3. **The product.** NS-500 is the Industrial Controller.
4. **The contract.** The agreement the request cites, ACME-MFG-2025, runs from **2025-04-01** to 2028-03-31. It was signed 2025-03-20/22, but it was not in force on 2025-03-31. The only agreement referenced for that date is ACME-SA-2023. I found it only through the older pricing exception; I did not find the agreement itself.
5. **The pricing exception.** The 15% exception, EXC-ACME-NS500-15, also starts on 2025-04-01. It replaces the older exception only "for requests dated on or after 2025-04-01." On 2025-03-31 the exception in force was **EXC-ACME-NS500-10**. It allows at most **10%** on NS-500 for orders from 2023-04-01 to 2025-03-31, and the request date is its last day. The requested 15% is above that cap.
6. **The policy.** Pricing Policy 2025 was in effect (2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31).
7. **Sarah's authority.** She is an Enterprise Account Executive. Under the 2025 policy she can approve discounts up to and including 15%. A 15% discount is within her own authority.
8. **The approver.** Since 15% is within her limit, no higher approver was needed for authority. If the discount had been above 15%, the 2025 policy would require VP Sales approval, which is Michael Torres.
9. **Decision.** Acme was only eligible for 10% that day. The SOP says a contract doesn't change who can approve, and approval authority can't make a customer eligible for more either. A higher approver wouldn't fix this. There are two ways forward:
   - Reduce the request to 10% or less. Sarah could then approve it herself under the 2025 policy and the 10% exception.
   - Redate the order to 2025-04-01 or later, when the new agreement and the 15% exception apply.

One point matters for how this is read: under the 2026 policy, Account Executive authority dropped to 10%. For DR-9001, a similar 2026 request, a 15% discount would need VP Sales approval.
