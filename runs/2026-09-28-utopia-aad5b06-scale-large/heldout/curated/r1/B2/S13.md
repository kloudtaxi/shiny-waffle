## Answer

**No, it can't be approved as submitted.** The Acme contract terms the request cites belong to a different Acme company, not to Acme Industrial Supply. The outcome is **REVIEW_REQUIRED**.

**What I found**

1. **The request:** DR-9105 asks for 15% off NS-500 (list $250,000, discount $37,500). Sarah Chen submitted it on 2026-09-23. The justification is "Contract pricing per Acme Master Supply Agreement", so it relies on special terms rather than standard pricing.
2. **The customer:** There are two separate Acme companies on record:
   - **Acme Industrial Supply Co.** is ERP customer C-1044, in Akron, OH (DUNS 09-552-1187). Its CRM segment is *Standard* and its account owner is **Zachary Brown**, not Sarah Chen. Nothing on record gives it a contract or pricing exception.
   - **Acme Manufacturing** is CRM segment *Strategic* and owned by Sarah Chen. It matches ERP customer C-1001, **Acme Mfg. Holdings**, in Milwaukee, WI (DUNS 04-812-7730). The Master Supply Agreement and the NS-500 exceptions belong to this company.
   - The account ID CRM-2091 isn't linked to either company in the knowledge base, so I couldn't confirm which one the CRM record means.
3. **The product:** NS-500 is the NS-500 Industrial Controller.
4. **Contract:** The Master Supply Agreement runs from 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31, so it was active on the request date. But it is Acme Mfg. Holdings' contract, not Acme Industrial Supply's.
5. **Pricing exception:** The "Acme NS-500 Strategic Account Exception" applies only to NS-500. It sets a maximum of **15%** from 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31 and replaces the earlier 10% exception (EXC-ACME-NS500-10). It was granted to Acme Mfg. Holdings. Special terms don't carry over to another company, so it doesn't cover Acme Industrial Supply.
6. **Policy:** Northstar Pricing Policy 2026 was in force on the request date:
   - up to 10%: the Enterprise Account Executive can approve;
   - over 10% up to 20%: needs the VP Sales;
   - over 20%: needs the Chief Revenue Officer.
7. **Sarah's authority:** Sarah Chen is an Enterprise Account Executive, so her limit is 10%. 15% is over it.
8. **Required approver:** 15% falls in the VP Sales band, and the VP Sales is **Michael Torres**, Sarah's manager.

**Decision**

As asked, for Acme Industrial Supply, this needs commercial review. That customer has no contract or exception that allows 15%. Having Michael Torres sign off wouldn't fix that, because the problem is the customer's eligibility, not Sarah's authority. Even a standard-pricing 15% would still need his approval.

Several signs point to Acme Manufacturing being the real customer: Sarah owns that account, she cited the Acme MSA, and her email says "Gina at Acme confirmed this morning they want to move ahead with the NS-500 controller for the third plant". If CRM-2091 does turn out to be Acme Manufacturing, the 15% is exactly at the exception's maximum. The outcome would then be **APPROVE_WITH_AUTHORIZATION**, with Michael Torres as VP Sales approving. The contract permits the discount, but it doesn't give Sarah authority above her 10% limit. The CRM record should be checked before anything is approved.
