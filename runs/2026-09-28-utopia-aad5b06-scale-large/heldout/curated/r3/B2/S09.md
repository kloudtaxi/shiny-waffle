## Question (verbatim)

> Could Sarah Chen have approved a 15% NS-500 discount for Acme Manufacturing on 2025-03-31? The request, as recorded in Northstar CRM: {"request_id": "DR-9101", "account_id": "CRM-2048", "product_sku": "NS-500", "requested_discount_pct": "15.0", "list_value_usd": "250000", "discount_value_usd": "37500", "net_value_usd": "212500", "requested_by": "sarah.chen@northstar.example", "request_date": "2025-03-31", "justification": "Contract pricing per Acme Master Supply Agreement", "status": "Pending Approval"}

## Answer

No. Sarah Chen could not have approved this. She had the authority for 15%, but Acme's contract did not allow 15% on NS-500 on 2025-03-31. The request asks for a discount above what Acme was entitled to that day, so it has to be rejected or escalated for new terms. It can't simply be approved.

1. **The request.** DR-9101 is for Acme Manufacturing (CRM-2048), product NS-500, 15% off $250,000 list ($37,500 off, $212,500 net). Sarah Chen requested it on 2025-03-31. It relies on special terms: "Contract pricing per Acme Master Supply Agreement." DR-9101 itself isn't in the knowledge base, so these details come from the record you gave me.
2. **The customer.** The knowledge base lists Sarah Chen as account owner of Acme Manufacturing. It also shows Acme's contract and exception terms under the ERP name "Acme Mfg. Holdings" (customer C-1001). I could not confirm from the data that CRM-2048 is that account, because the knowledge base holds no facts about CRM-2048.
3. **The product.** NS-500 is the NS-500 Industrial Controller.
4. **The contract on 2025-03-31.** The Master Supply Agreement the request cites was effective from 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31. It was **not yet in force** on the request date. The earlier agreement, in force from 2023-04-01 to 2025-03-31, allowed Acme up to **10%** off NS-500.
5. **The pricing exception on 2025-03-31.** EXC-ACME-NS500-10 set the NS-500 maximum at **10%** of list, valid 2023-04-01 to 2025-03-31. The 15% "Acme NS-500 Strategic Account Exception" replaces it, but only from **2025-04-01**. David Morgan approved it on 2025-03-20, but approval doesn't make it valid earlier. So the request was filed one day before the 15% terms began.
6. **The policy.** Northstar Pricing Policy 2025 was in force (2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31).
7. **Sarah Chen's authority.** She is an Enterprise Account Executive. Under the 2025 policy, that role can approve up to and including 15%, so authority was not the problem. Under the 2026 policy the limit would be 10%, but that policy doesn't apply here.
8. **The required approver.** The 2025 policy requires VP Sales only above 15%. Since the obstacle is Acme's entitlement rather than anyone's authority, a VP Sales sign-off wouldn't fix it.
9. **Decision: REJECT_OR_ESCALATE.** On the request date Acme could get at most 10%, and 15% goes over that. It can't be approved without new or amended terms. The simplest fix is to reissue the request dated on or after 2025-04-01. The 15% ceiling applies from then, and under the 2025 policy Sarah Chen could approve 15% herself.
