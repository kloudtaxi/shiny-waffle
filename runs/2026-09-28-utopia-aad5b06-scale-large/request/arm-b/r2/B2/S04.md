## Answer

**Yes.** On 2025-09-23 Sarah Chen could have approved DR-9001 herself. Acme was allowed up to 15% on the NS-500 then, and the 2025 policy let an Enterprise Account Executive approve discounts up to and including 15%.

**Steps**

1. **The request.** DR-9001 is for 15% off $250,000 list, giving $212,500 net. Sarah Chen requested it on 2025-09-23. It relies on special terms: "Contract pricing per Acme Master Supply Agreement."
2. **The customer.** The CRM account is "Acme Manufacturing," and Sarah Chen is its account owner. The ERP customer is C-1001, "Acme Mfg. Holdings." The request's account ID, CRM-2048, isn't linked to anything in the knowledge base. The graph also has no direct link between the two Acme names. One source document uses both names, and the Acme terms are recorded under both, so I've treated them as the same customer.
3. **The product.** SKU NS-500 is the NS-500 Industrial Controller.
4. **An active contract.** There is an entity called "Master Supply Agreement," but it has no facts: no dates, terms or customer link. What establishes eligibility is the pricing exception below. I couldn't check the agreement's own wording.
5. **A pricing exception.** The "Acme NS-500 Strategic Account Exception" covers this date. It allows up to 15% off list from 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31 and applies only to the NS-500 Industrial Controller. David Morgan approved it on 2025-03-20. It replaced EXC-ACME-NS500-10, which capped the discount at 10% until 2025-03-31. The request is at the 15% maximum, not above it, so Acme is eligible.
6. **The policy.** Northstar Pricing Policy 2025 was in force from 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31.
7. **The requestor's authority.** Sarah Chen is an Enterprise Account Executive. Under the 2025 policy that role can approve discounts up to and including 15%, so 15% is within her authority. One gap: her job title has no date in the records, so I've assumed she held it in September 2025.
8. **The required approver.** None beyond Sarah. The 2025 policy only requires VP Sales approval above 15%.
9. **Decision: APPROVE.** Acme was eligible for 15%, and the discount was within her own limit.

The date matters. Under the 2026 policy, an Enterprise Account Executive's limit drops to 10%, and discounts above 10% up to 20% need VP Sales approval. The same request made in 2026 would therefore be APPROVE_WITH_AUTHORIZATION, not APPROVE. The exception gives Acme the right to the discount, but it doesn't give any employee authority to approve it.
