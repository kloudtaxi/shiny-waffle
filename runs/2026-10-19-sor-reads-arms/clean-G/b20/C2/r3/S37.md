## Question (verbatim)

> Ticket SR-39207 (Cedar Health, NS-Cloud unavailable at two facilities): what is its true severity, was a service level breached, what does Northstar owe, who must be engaged or notified and by when, and what must the customer do?

## Answer

**SR-39207 is not a breach.** Its true severity is **Severity 2**, both service levels were met, and no credit is owed. Northstar does still owe Cedar Health a written decision on its credit claim.

**Customer and scope.** The ticket is on CRM account CRM-2063, Cedar Health Systems. That account carries DUNS 02-906-4418, the same DUNS as ERP customer C-1003, Cedar Health Systems Inc. Its support terms are on the Silver plan and cover NS-Cloud, with coverage starting 2025-12-01. The ticket was opened 2026-05-22, so it is in scope.

**True severity: Severity 2.** The recorded impact is that NS-Cloud was down at 2 of 14 facilities (the Fishers and Carmel outpatient centers), while "the other 12 facilities are working normally." The severity guide makes it Severity 2 when NS-Cloud is "unavailable to some, but not all, of the customer's users or sites." That matches the customer's reported P2. The knowledge base only holds the guide's Severity 1 heading, not its criteria. I relied on the matching Severity 2 criterion and on the outage being partial.

**Schedule and targets.** The ticket opened at 2026-05-22 15:20 CT, so Schedule v2.0 applies (tickets opened on or after 2026-04-01). For Silver, Severity 2 the targets are a 2-business-hour response and a 12-business-hour restoration. Business hours are 08:00–18:00 CT, Monday to Friday. Monday 2026-05-25 (Memorial Day) is a Northstar holiday. No pauses, maintenance windows or exclusions are recorded, and the defaults are no pause and no exclusion.

**Measurement (all times CT):**
- **Initial response: met.** The clock started at Friday 15:20 CT, and the engineer responded at 17:10 CT (18:10 −04:00). That is 1 h 50 m against a 2 h target.
- **Restoration: met.** Service was restored Tuesday 2026-05-26 at 17:05 CT (18:05 −04:00). The clock counts Friday 15:20–18:00 (2 h 40 m), skips the weekend and Memorial Day, then counts Tuesday 08:00–17:05 (9 h 05 m). That is 11 h 45 m against a 12 h target, which would have run out Tuesday at 17:20 CT. The customer's "down for four days" is calendar time, which a business-hours clock doesn't count.

**Credits.** The amount is $0.00, because no target was missed. The claim itself was valid: received in writing via the portal on 2026-06-01 at 08:30 CT. That is inside the window, which runs 30 days after the restoration date, to 2026-06-25 23:59 CT. The monthly cap (30% of the $4,000 fee, so $1,200) doesn't come into play.

**What Northstar owes:**
- **Written claim decision.** Marcus Adeyemi, Director of Customer Support, must decide the claim by 2026-07-01, the 30th calendar day after receipt. The decision should deny it, since no target was missed. That date has passed, and the record doesn't show whether a decision was sent. Someone should check this.
- **No credit memo.** No credit was approved, so Paul Brennan (Support Billing Manager) has nothing to issue.
- **No escalations or notifications came due.** The response was on time, and service was restored before the restoration target ran out (the point at which the Director would have had to be notified). The Severity 1 steps don't apply.
- **No root-cause finding is due,** because no exclusion was asserted.

**What the customer must do:**
- **Credit claim:** done on time.
- **Telephone report:** not required, because that rule applies only to Severity 1.
- **Information and access:** Northstar recorded no requests, so nothing was outstanding.
