## Answer

**SR-39644 is Severity 2, not P1. No service level was breached, so Northstar owes no credit and no escalation was triggered.** The restoration target was met exactly at its 12-hour limit.

**Customer and scope.** The ticket is on CRM account CRM-2048 (Acme Manufacturing, Strategic tier, DUNS 04-812-7730). That DUNS matches ERP customer C-1001, Acme Mfg. Holdings. Acme's Schedule C gives it the Platinum plan, with coverage from 2025-04-01 00:00 CT, and lists the NS-Edge Monitoring Package (NS-EDGE) as a covered product. The ticket is in scope.

**True severity: 2.** The record says the NS-Edge gateways stopped sending vibration data for presses 1–6 of 14. The presses are still running, and technicians are doing manual vibration checks every two hours. The severity guide puts two matching conditions in Severity 2: a condition-monitoring function that is unavailable, and a Severity 1 condition with a workaround that needs ongoing manual effort. The customer's P1 is only their view and doesn't set the severity.

**Rules that apply.** The ticket opened at 2026-07-23 13:10 CDT, so Schedule v2.0 applies (it covers tickets opened from 2026-04-01). For Severity 2 on Platinum, the clock runs 24×7 and starts at the open time. The targets are a 2-hour initial response and a 12-hour restoration.

**Measurement (all times CDT, −05:00):**
- **Initial response:** an engineer replied on 23 Jul at 15:00, 1 h 50 min after opening. The 2-hour target is **met**.
- **Pause:** at 15:00 on 23 Jul the engineer recorded a request for VPN credentials and set the ticket to Awaiting Customer. Johnny Johnson recorded the access as provided at 08:30 on 24 Jul. Both ends are recorded, so the 17 h 30 min pause counts. It stops only the restoration clock.
- **Restoration:** restored on 24 Jul at 18:40. Time counted is 13:10–15:00 (1 h 50 min) plus 08:30–18:40 (10 h 10 min), which is **exactly 12 h 00 min**. A target is missed only if the time is more than 12 hours, so it is **met**.
- **Exclusions:** none. No maintenance notices are recorded for CRM-2048, and Northstar did not assert the customer-caused exclusion.

**What hinges on the pause.** The 15:00 note already says Kelly Ballard (Acme IT Architecture) "provided them", but gives no time for that. The schedule ends a pause when the access is recorded, and the only recorded time is 08:30 on 24 Jul. If the pause were cut short or rejected, restoration would be over 12 hours and become a breach. Without the pause, it would be 29 h 30 min, which is more than twice the target and a band 2 miss.

**Credits.** None are owed, because no target was missed. No credit claim is recorded either. For reference only:

- The two credits already approved for Acme were for March 2026 tickets, so they don't count toward the July cap.

**What Northstar owes.** No escalation was triggered:

- **Restoration:** if the ticket had still been open at 18:40 on 24 Jul, the Director of Customer Support (Marcus Adeyemi) and the account owner (Sarah Chen) would have had to be notified. It was restored at that moment, so this did not apply.
- **Severity 1 steps don't apply:** these were engaging the Support Escalation Manager within 15 minutes and notifying the CRO for Strategic accounts.
- **Claims and root cause:** there is no claim to decide, no credit memo for the Support Billing Manager (Paul Brennan), and no root-cause finding due from the Support Engineering Lead (Owen Takahashi).
- **Unconfirmed rule:** the escalation procedure also has a rule to notify the account owner within 10 business hours of the open time. I couldn't confirm which tickets it covers. It may be the out-of-scope handling, which wouldn't apply here.

**What the customer must do.**

- **Phone report:** reporting by phone to the Support hotline is required only for Severity 1, so it doesn't apply. Their portal report was fine.
- **Credit claim:** none is needed, because no credit can arise.
