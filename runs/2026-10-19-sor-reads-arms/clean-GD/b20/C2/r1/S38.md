## Answer

# SR-39561: BlueRiver Logistics, NS-Cloud down for all users. Outcome: **NO_BREACH**

No service level was breached, and no credit is owed. The outage began during a planned NS-Cloud maintenance window that BlueRiver had been notified about. The clocks therefore start when the window closed, at 02:00, and Northstar met both targets measured from then.

**1. Customer.** Matched by identifiers, not by name. The ticket is on CRM account CRM-2051 (BlueRiver Logistics, DUNS 07-331-9025). That DUNS matches ERP customer C-1002, "Blue River Logistics LLC". The account's CRM tier is Standard, and its account owner is Kelly Floyd.

**2. Scope: in.** BlueRiver's support terms (SUP-BRL-2025) cover NS-Cloud Operations Suite (NS-CLOUD) on the Silver plan. Coverage began 2025-01-01 00:00 CT, and the monthly support fee is $2,500.

**3. True severity: Severity 1.** The ticket says NS-Cloud was "down for all our users", dispatch could not see any shipments, and there was "No workaround". The severity guide assigns the first severity that the recorded impact meets. The knowledge base doesn't hold the guide's exact Severity 1 wording, but a total outage with no workaround meets the top level. The customer's reported P1 was not used to decide this.

**4. Schedule and targets.** The ticket was opened 2026-07-11 at 23:20 CT, so Schedule v2.0 applies (it covers tickets opened from 2026-04-01). Severity 1 runs on a 24×7 clock on every plan. The targets are an initial response within 30 minutes and restoration within 4 hours.

**5. Clock start: 2026-07-12T02:00-05:00.**
- **The maintenance window qualifies.** Notice MN-2607 names NS-CLOUD and gives the window as 2026-07-11 22:00 to 2026-07-12 02:00 CT. It was sent 2026-06-26, at least 9 business days before the window, and the rule requires 5.
- **The clock starts when the window ends.** A ticket opened inside a qualifying window starts both clocks at the window's end.
- **This holds even though the upgrade caused the outage.** The engineer's note blames the MN-2607 upgrade, but the schedule says time after the window counts normally even if the maintenance caused the incident.
- **No pauses, and no other exclusion.** None were recorded, and Northstar asserted no customer-caused exclusion.

**6. Measurement.**
- **Response: met.** An engineer phoned at 23:35, before the clock started. The schedule treats a response before clock start as happening at time zero, so the 30-minute target is met.
- **Restoration: met.** Service came back at 05:05, which is 3 h 05 min after 02:00 and inside the 4-hour target.

**7. Credits: $0.** BlueRiver's claim (received by email 2026-07-14, naming the ticket) was in writing and on time; the claim deadline was 2026-08-11. But no target was missed, so nothing is owed. The claim counts the outage as 5 h 45 min, from 23:20, which ignores the maintenance window. The rate would have been 10% of the $2,500 fee (Severity 1, band 1) if 4 hours had been exceeded, but it wasn't.

- **No other notifications.** The VP Sales notice wasn't triggered because service was restored before the 4-hour mark (06:00). The Chief Revenue Officer isn't notified for a Standard-tier account.
- **No credit memo and no root-cause finding** are due.
- **Gaps in the record:**

  - **Claim decision.** No decision on the claim is recorded, and its 2026-08-13 deadline has passed. Northstar still owes BlueRiver that written decision.

**9. What the customer had to do: both duties were met.**
- **Telephone report.** BlueRiver reported the Severity 1 by phone before restoration: the night dispatch supervisor called at 23:20.
- **Credit claim.** BlueRiver claimed in writing on time. The claim fails because nothing was breached, not because it was late.
- **Reachable contact.** BlueRiver had to keep a technical contact reachable by phone. The ticket records no failed attempt to reach them.
