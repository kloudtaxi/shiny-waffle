## Answer

**No, he shouldn't approve it.** Michael Torres is VP Sales, and that role can sign off on an 18% discount. But Acme's contract only allows up to 15% on the NS-500, so this request goes beyond what the customer is entitled to. A more senior approver can't fix that. It needs new or amended contract terms.

**How I checked it**
1. **Request:** DR-9112 asks for 18% off NS-500 for CRM-2048 on 2026-09-23. It relies on the Acme Master Supply Agreement, so it depends on Acme's contract terms. I used the record as you supplied it and didn't find it in the knowledge base's CRM request export.
2. **Customer:** CRM-2048 "Acme Manufacturing" is the ERP customer Acme Mfg. Holdings (C-1001), which does business as Acme Manufacturing. Acme Industrial Supply Co. (CRM-2091 / C-1044) is a different company, and none of its terms apply here.
3. **Contract:** Agreement ACME-MFG-2025 is with Acme Mfg. Holdings and runs from 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31, so it was active on the request date. It covers the NS-500.
4. **Pricing exception:** EXC-ACME-NS500-15 (Schedule B to that agreement) covers the NS-500 only, from 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31, with a **maximum discount of 15% of list price**. It replaced the earlier 10% exception.
5. **Policy:** Northstar Pricing Policy 2026 and its Approval Authority Matrix are in force. Account executives can approve up to 10%, VP Sales more than 10% up to 20%, and the Chief Revenue Officer above 20%.
6. **Authority:** Michael Torres is listed as VP Sales, so 18% is within his limit. But both the contract (§3.3) and the exception say that authority and eligibility are separate. Having the authority doesn't make the customer eligible for a larger discount.

**Next step:** Acme would need an amended or new pricing exception above 15%. The current one was approved by David Morgan, the Chief Revenue Officer. Otherwise the request should come down to 15% or less, which Michael could then approve. DR-9001, Sarah Chen's pending 15% request for the same deal, already fits those limits.
