## Answer

No, not on the evidence we have. Authority isn't the problem: Sarah Chen was allowed to approve 8%. The problem is that the agreement the request cites wasn't in force yet on 2025-03-31. The right step is to ask for the contract that did apply that day before deciding.

**1. The request.** DR-9110 asks for 8% off NS-500 for CRM-2048, dated 2025-03-31. Its only stated reason is "Contract pricing per Acme Master Supply Agreement", so it relies on special contract terms rather than standard pricing.

**2. The customer.** CRM-2048 "Acme Manufacturing" is ERP customer C-1001, Acme Mfg. Holdings. The address and DUNS number (04-812-7730) match. It is a different company from Acme Industrial Supply Co. (CRM-2091 / C-1044, Akron).

**3. The cited agreement wasn't in force on the request date.** The Master Supply Agreement (ACME-MFG-2025, with Acme Mfg. Holdings doing business as Acme Manufacturing) runs from 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31. It was signed on 2025-03-20/22 but didn't take effect until the day after the request. The same goes for its 15% NS-500 exception (EXC-ACME-NS500-15), which also starts 2025-04-01.

**4. The terms that did cover that date rest on a contract we can't find.** The earlier exception, EXC-ACME-NS500-10, allowed "Acme Manufacturing" up to 10% on NS-500 for orders from 2023-04-01 to 2025-03-31. That window includes the request date, and 8% is under the cap. But that exception belongs to a different agreement, ACME-SA-2023, which is not in the knowledge base. Nothing confirms that agreement was active with Acme Mfg. Holdings on 2025-03-31, and the request doesn't cite it anyway. Our procedure says that when a request relies on a contract term that can't be found, we ask for the document before deciding.

**5. Authority wasn't an issue.** The 2025 Pricing Policy was in force that day. It let Enterprise Account Executives approve up to 15%, and Sarah Chen is one. (The 2026 policy's 10% limit doesn't apply to requests dated in 2025.) Her authority doesn't settle it, though: the contract says customer eligibility and internal approval authority are separate questions, and both have to be established.

**Next step:** ask for the ACME-SA-2023 agreement, or other proof that an active contract with Acme Mfg. Holdings covered 2025-03-31. If it turns up, 8% is within both the 10% exception and her 15% limit, so she could approve it herself.
