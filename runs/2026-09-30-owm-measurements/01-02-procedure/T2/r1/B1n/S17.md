## Answer

No, she can't approve it. There are two separate problems, and the first one means it needs commercial review before anyone approves it.

**1. The Acme contract doesn't give Acme a discount on NS-EDGE.** The justification cites the Acme Master Supply Agreement (ACME-MFG-2025).
- **Customer:** CRM account CRM-2048 is Acme Mfg. Holdings, doing business as Acme Manufacturing, ERP customer C-1001. This is not "Acme Industrial" (CRM-2091), a different customer that also uses NS-EDGE.
- **Contract:** The agreement runs from 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31, so it was in force on 2026-09-23. It lists NS-EDGE among the products Northstar supplies to Acme.
- **Discount terms:** The only discount term (§3.2) is up to 15% on the NS-500 Industrial Controller, set out in pricing exception EXC-ACME-NS500-15. That exception says it applies to NS-500 only and "does not apply to any other Northstar product." For NS-EDGE, §3.1 just says list price less any discount agreed in an order.
- **Other exceptions:** I found none that covers NS-EDGE for Acme.

So the discount this request relies on doesn't exist for this product. Acme's NS-500 terms can't be carried over to NS-EDGE.

**2. 12% is above Sarah's own approval limit.** The Northstar Pricing Policy 2026 and the Approval Authority Matrix apply on this date. Sarah is an Enterprise Account Executive, and that role can approve up to 10%. Discounts over 10% and up to 20% need the VP Sales, currently Michael Torres. Even with a valid contract basis, she couldn't approve 12% herself. The 15% limit in the 2025 policy no longer applies. Both the agreement (§3.3) and the exception also say that contract terms don't grant anyone approval authority.

**Next steps:** Deal Desk needs to decide whether Acme gets new or amended terms for NS-EDGE. The other route is to resubmit the request as a standard or competitive discount rather than contract pricing. Either way, 12% still needs Michael Torres's approval, and approvals above 10% must be recorded in Northstar CRM.
