> Could Sarah Chen have approved a 15% NS-500 discount for Acme Manufacturing on 2025-03-31? The request, as recorded in Northstar CRM: {"request_id": "DR-9101", "account_id": "CRM-2048", "product_sku": "NS-500", "requested_discount_pct": "15.0", "list_value_usd": "250000", "discount_value_usd": "37500", "net_value_usd": "212500", "requested_by": "sarah.chen@northstar.example", "request_date": "2025-03-31", "justification": "Contract pricing per Acme Master Supply Agreement", "status": "Pending Approval"}

## Answer

No. Sarah had enough authority on that date, but Acme wasn't entitled to 15% yet. It could get at most 10%, so she couldn't approve DR-9101 as submitted.

**Her authority was enough.** The 2025 pricing policy was in force on 2025-03-31. It lets an Enterprise Account Executive, which is Sarah Chen's role, approve up to and including 15%. So a 15% discount was within her limit. (Under the 2026 policy the limit fell to 10%, but that doesn't apply to a 2025 date.)

**Acme's entitlement was the problem.** The request cites the Acme Master Supply Agreement:
- That agreement (ACME-MFG-2025) is with Acme Mfg. Holdings, trading as Acme Manufacturing. It gives 15% on NS-500, but it only takes effect on 2025-04-01. The 15% pricing exception attached to it, EXC-ACME-NS500-15, starts on 2025-04-01 too. On 2025-03-31 neither was in force.
- What did cover NS-500 that day was the 2023 exception, EXC-ACME-NS500-10, under agreement ACME-SA-2023. It allows up to **10%** on orders placed from 2023-04-01 to 2025-03-31.
- 15% is more than that 10% maximum. A more senior approver can't fix this; it needs new or changed terms. Being within her authority doesn't matter here. The procedure says a customer contract doesn't change who may approve, and an employee's authority doesn't create an entitlement the customer doesn't have.

**What she could do:** approve up to 10% herself on 2025-03-31, or date the request on or after 2025-04-01, when the 15% terms apply.

**One thing to check:** the 2023 agreement itself (ACME-SA-2023) isn't in the knowledge base. The 10% limit comes only from the 2023 exception, which calls that agreement active and names "Acme Manufacturing". If you need the legal entity confirmed, get that agreement.
