## Answer

Yes. If the request date really is 2025-09-23, Sarah Chen could have approved this 15% discount herself. There is one date problem you should check first, though. The request in Northstar CRM's discount_requests.csv shows DR-9001 dated **2026-09-23**, not 2025-09-23. That changes who can approve it.

**Checks for 2025-09-23:**
1. **Customer:** CRM account CRM-2048, Acme Manufacturing, is legally Acme Mfg. Holdings doing business as Acme Manufacturing. Sarah Chen is the account owner.
2. **Contract:** The Acme Master Supply Agreement (ACME-MFG-2025) runs from 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31, so it was active on that date. It covers NS-500.
3. **Pricing exception:** Exception EXC-ACME-NS500-15 is Schedule B to that agreement. It allows up to 15% off list, for NS-500 only, from 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31. It replaced the older 10% exception (EXC-ACME-NS500-10), which expired on 2025-03-31. The requested 15% is exactly at the maximum.
4. **Policy:** Northstar Pricing Policy 2025 was in force for all of 2025. Under it, Enterprise Account Executives may approve discounts up to and including 15%.
5. **Authority:** Sarah Chen is an Enterprise Account Executive (employee EMP-101). So 15% was within her own limit. That authority comes from the policy, not from the contract. The agreement's §3.3 says the customer's pricing eligibility doesn't change Northstar's internal approval authority.

There's a precedent: DR-8104 was the same request (15%, $250,000, Acme Master Supply Agreement) from Sarah Chen, dated 2025-09-16, and it was marked Approved.

**If the date is 2026-09-23, as CRM records it:** Acme is still eligible for 15%. But Pricing Policy 2026 and the 2026 Approval Authority Matrix cut Enterprise Account Executive authority to 10% or less. Discounts over 10% and up to 20% need the VP Sales, Michael Torres. Sarah could not self-approve, and the outcome would be `APPROVE_WITH_AUTHORIZATION`.
