## Answer

**Yes, Sarah Chen could have approved it herself, but the request cites the wrong agreement.**

**Her authority:** The pricing policy in force on 2025-03-31 was the Northstar Pricing Policy 2025 (1 Jan – 31 Dec 2025). Under it, Enterprise Account Executives can approve discounts up to and including 15%. Sarah Chen is an Enterprise Account Executive and owns the CRM-2048 account. 8% is well inside that limit, so no VP Sales or CRO sign-off was needed. (Even the stricter 2026 limit of 10% would have covered it.)

**The contract basis is the problem:** The request says "Contract pricing per Acme Master Supply Agreement." That agreement (ACME-MFG-2025) and its 15% NS-500 exception (EXC-ACME-NS500-15) only start on 2025-04-01. On 2025-03-31 neither was in force yet, so the reason given in the request doesn't hold for that date.

**What actually covered that date:** An earlier exception, EXC-ACME-NS500-10 (under agreement ACME-SA-2023), lets Acme Manufacturing have up to 10% off NS-500 list price on orders placed from 2023-04-01 to 2025-03-31. The newer exception replaces it only for requests dated 2025-04-01 or later. So on 2025-03-31, its last day, Acme was entitled to 8%.

**Before recording the approval:**
- Change the justification to cite EXC-ACME-NS500-10 / ACME-SA-2023 instead of the Master Supply Agreement.
- The ACME-SA-2023 agreement itself isn't in the knowledge base. Only the exception document is, and it says "Status: Active." If the exception is questioned, get a copy of that agreement.
- The 8% figure is consistent: $20,000 off a $250,000 list price.
