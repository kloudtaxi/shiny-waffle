## Answer

Yes. If the request was dated 2025-09-23, Sarah Chen could have approved the 15% discount herself. But Northstar CRM records DR-9001 with a date of **2026-09-23**, not 2025-09-23, and under the 2026 rules she could not approve it alone.

**If the date is 2025-09-23:**
1. **Customer:** CRM account CRM-2048 is Acme Manufacturing, and Sarah Chen owns the account. The contract names the customer as Acme Mfg. Holdings, doing business as Acme Manufacturing.
2. **Product:** NS-500 Industrial Controller.
3. **Contract:** The Master Supply Agreement (ACME-MFG-2025) runs from 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31, so it was active on that date. Section 3.3 says the contract doesn't change Northstar's internal approval rules.
4. **Pricing exception:** EXC-ACME-NS500-15 lets Acme get up to 15% off list price on NS-500 only, from 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31. It replaced an earlier 10% exception for requests dated on or after 2025-04-01. The exception covers what the customer can get, not who can approve it. The 15% request is at the maximum, so Acme is eligible.
5. **Policy:** The Northstar Pricing Policy 2025 applies to that date. It lets Enterprise Account Executives approve discounts up to and including 15%; anything above 15% needs the VP Sales.
6. **Authority:** Sarah Chen is an Enterprise Account Executive, so 15% is within her own limit.

The CRM has a similar request, DR-8104 (same account, product, 15% and justification, dated 2025-09-16), which is marked Approved.

**At the date in the CRM (2026-09-23):** The Northstar Pricing Policy 2026 lowered the Enterprise Account Executive limit to 10%. Discounts above 10% and up to 20% need the VP Sales, Michael Torres. Acme is still eligible for 15% under the exception, but that doesn't give Sarah Chen more authority. The result would be approval only with Michael Torres's sign-off. It's worth checking which date is correct before anyone acts on the request.
