## Answer

I've checked the contracts, the pricing exceptions and the 2025 policy. Here's what I found.

**Short answer: Yes, she could have approved it herself. But the justification cites the wrong document and should be corrected first.**

**1. The request and customer.** DR-9110 is from account CRM-2048, Acme Manufacturing. In the ERP system that account is Acme Mfg. Holdings (C-1001), with the same DUNS number and address. Sarah Chen owns the account. The product is SKU NS-500, the Industrial Controller.

**2. The contract cited isn't in force yet.** The justification points to the Acme Master Supply Agreement (ACME-MFG-2025). That agreement starts on **2025-04-01**, one day after this request, and runs to 2028-03-31. Its 15% exception (EXC-ACME-NS500-15) also only starts on 2025-04-01. So neither the agreement nor its 15% exception covers a request dated 2025-03-31.

**3. An older exception does cover it.** EXC-ACME-NS500-10, which falls under an earlier agreement (ACME-SA-2023), allows up to **10%** off list on NS-500 only, for orders from 2023-04-01 to **2025-03-31**. The request date is the last day it applies. 8% is under that 10% cap, so Acme was eligible for the discount.

**4. Sarah has the authority.** The Northstar Pricing Policy 2025 was in force from 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31. Under it, Enterprise Account Executives can approve discounts up to and including 15%. Sarah Chen is an Enterprise Account Executive who reports to Michael Torres, VP Sales. 8% is within her limit, so no higher approver is needed. The stricter 10% limit for account executives only begins in 2026. Even with no special terms at all, 8% would still be within her authority.

**Caveats:**
- The CRM record should cite EXC-ACME-NS500-10 / ACME-SA-2023, not the Master Supply Agreement. The approval shouldn't rest on an agreement that wasn't yet in force.
- I couldn't find the ACME-SA-2023 agreement itself in the knowledge base, only the exception, which is marked "Status: Active".
- Sarah's job title comes from current HR and org-chart records, which have no dates. I'm assuming she held the same role in March 2025.
- The exception ends on 2025-03-31. That should also be the date the order is placed, not just the date of the request.
