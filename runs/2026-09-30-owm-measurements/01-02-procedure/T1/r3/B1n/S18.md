## Answer

Yes. Sarah Chen could have approved the 8% discount herself on 2025-03-31. No higher approver was needed. One thing should be fixed: the request cites the wrong agreement.

**The request (DR-9110):** Acme Manufacturing (CRM-2048), NS-500 Industrial Controller, 8% off list ($20,000 on $250,000), requested by Sarah Chen on 2025-03-31. The stated basis is "Contract pricing per Acme Master Supply Agreement."

**Is Acme eligible for the discount?** Yes, but not under the agreement the request names.
- The Acme Master Supply Agreement (ACME-MFG-2025) and its 15% NS-500 exception (EXC-ACME-NS500-15) only take effect on 2025-04-01. That is one day after the request, so neither applied on 2025-03-31.
- The earlier exception, EXC-ACME-NS500-10 (under agreement ACME-SA-2023), still applied that day. It allows Acme up to 10% off NS-500 list price for orders placed from 2023-04-01 to 2025-03-31. 8% is within that 10%, and the exception covers this product and date. The new exception only replaces it for requests dated on or after 2025-04-01.

**Could Sarah approve it herself?** Yes.
- The Northstar Pricing Policy 2025 applied (2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31). Under it, Enterprise Account Executives may approve discounts up to and including 15%. Anything above 15% needs VP Sales approval.
- The org chart lists Sarah Chen as an Enterprise Account Executive. It isn't dated, so I'm assuming she held that role on 2025-03-31.
- 8% is within her 15% limit, so she didn't need VP Sales (Michael Torres).
- Her authority comes from the policy, not from the contract. Both the MSA (§3.3) and the exception state that contract terms don't grant approval authority.

**Caveats:**
- The justification should be changed to cite EXC-ACME-NS500-10 / ACME-SA-2023 instead of the Master Supply Agreement. If the request is re-dated to 2025-04-01 or later, it falls under the new agreement and 15% exception instead, and 8% would still be covered.
- The ACME-SA-2023 agreement itself isn't in the knowledge base. Only the exception document that references it is, and it shows the exception as active.
- The 2023 exception says orders placed "from 2023-04-01 to 2025-03-31". I've read that as including 2025-03-31.
- Even without any exception, 8% is within Sarah's standard 15% limit, so the outcome would be the same.
