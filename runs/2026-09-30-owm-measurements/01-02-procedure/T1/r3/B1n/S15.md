## Answer

**No, not on this basis.** The outcome is **REVIEW_REQUIRED**. Michael Torres has enough authority for a 12% discount, but the only reason the request gives is a contract that belongs to a different customer.

**What I checked**

1. **The request.** DR-9107 asks for 12% off NS-500 for BlueRiver Logistics (CRM-2051) on 2026-09-23. That's $30,000 off a $250,000 list price. Michael Torres submitted it. The stated reason is "Contract pricing per Acme Master Supply Agreement," so it relies on special contract terms, not standard pricing.
2. **The customer.** BlueRiver Logistics is ERP customer C-1002, "Blue River Logistics LLC" (DUNS 07-331-9025, St. Louis, MO). It's a Standard-tier account owned by Kelly Floyd. It is not Acme.
3. **The contract.** The Acme agreement (ACME-MFG-2025) is between Northstar and Acme Mfg. Holdings, trading as Acme Manufacturing (C-1001 / CRM-2048). Its NS-500 pricing exception (EXC-ACME-NS500-15, up to 15%, valid 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31) names Acme Manufacturing as the customer. I found no contract or pricing exception for BlueRiver. Special terms don't transfer to another customer.
4. **Policy and authority.** The Northstar Pricing Policy 2026 and the 2026 Approval Authority Matrix apply on the request date. Enterprise Account Executives can approve up to 10%. VP Sales approves above 10% up to 20%, and the Chief Revenue Officer above 20%. Michael Torres is VP Sales, so 12% is within his authority.
5. **Decision.** Authority alone isn't enough. The procedure only allows approval once the customer's eligibility for special terms is established, and here it isn't. The request needs commercial review. It could be resubmitted as a standard-pricing request, in which case Michael Torres could approve 12% himself, or backed by terms that actually cover BlueRiver. One more point: he is both the requestor and the approver, which you may want looked at during that review.
