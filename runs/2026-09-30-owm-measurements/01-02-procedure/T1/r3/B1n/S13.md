## Answer

**No, DR-9105 can't be approved as submitted. It needs commercial review (`REVIEW_REQUIRED`).** The Acme agreement it cites, and the 15% NS-500 pricing, belong to a different customer called Acme Manufacturing. Nothing on file gives Acme Industrial Supply Co. those terms.

**Why the terms don't apply**
- **The customer is different.** CRM-2091 is Acme Industrial Supply Co. (ERP C-1044): Akron, OH, D-U-N-S 09-552-1187, account owner Zachary Brown. The "Acme Master Supply Agreement" (ACME-MFG-2025) is with Acme Mfg. Holdings, which trades as Acme Manufacturing (CRM-2048 / ERP C-1001): Milwaukee, WI, D-U-N-S 04-812-7730, account owner Sarah Chen.
- **The pricing exception is limited too.** EXC-ACME-NS500-15 allows up to 15% on NS-500 from 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31, but only for Acme Manufacturing. Special terms don't carry over to another account just because the name is similar.
- I found no contract or pricing exception for Acme Industrial Supply Co. The only other document about that account is a support ticket (SR-40418), which says no change to commercial terms was requested.
- **This may be a mix-up.** On the same date, Sarah Chen filed an identical request under the real Acme Manufacturing account (DR-9001: NS-500, 15%, $250,000 list value, same justification). DR-9105 may have been entered on the wrong account by mistake, so it's worth checking with her.

**Approval authority, which applies whatever the pricing basis**
- The Northstar Pricing Policy 2026 and the 2026 Approval Authority Matrix were in force on 2026-09-23. Under them, an Enterprise Account Executive (Sarah Chen's role) can approve up to 10%.
- The 2025 policy's 15% limit no longer applies.
- A 15% discount needs VP Sales approval (the band above 10% up to 20%). That's Michael Torres.
- Even if Acme Industrial Supply is found eligible, Sarah Chen can't approve this herself. A customer contract never changes an employee's approval authority.
