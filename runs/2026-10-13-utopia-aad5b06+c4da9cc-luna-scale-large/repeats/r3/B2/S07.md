## Question (verbatim)

> Why is Acme Manufacturing eligible for a 15% discount on the NS-500, and what is the evidence?

## Answer

I found the pricing exception document and its details, so I'm writing up the answer now.

**Answer:** Acme Manufacturing can get up to 15% off the NS-500 because of a signed pricing exception, **EXC-ACME-NS500-15**, recorded in `acme_pricing_exception.md`. It does not come from Acme's main supply agreement.

**What the exception says:**
- **Customer:** Acme Manufacturing (it also appears in the records as Acme Mfg. Holdings, CRM-2048, ERP C-1001).
- **Product:** "NS-500 Industrial Controller (NS-500) only." It "does not apply to any other Northstar product."
- **Maximum eligible discount:** 15% of list price. The NS-500 lists at $250,000.
- **Effective dates:** 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31, so it is in force today (2026-10-13).
- **Approved by:** David Morgan, Chief Revenue Officer, on 2025-03-20. Prepared by Sarah Chen.
- **What it replaces:** "Supersedes EXC-ACME-NS500-10 for requests dated on or after 2025-04-01." That older exception, tied to agreement ACME-SA-2023, allowed **10%** from 2023-04-01 to 2025-03-31.

**Two limits on the exception:**
1. **It only makes Acme eligible.** The document says it "establishes commercial eligibility only" and "does not grant approval authority to any Northstar employee." A 15% deal still needs normal discount approval. Another document mentions Deal Desk confirming the preferential 15% NS-500 pricing.
2. **The 15% is a maximum.** Acme can be offered anything up to 15%, but isn't automatically owed the full amount.

**A point to correct:** In `email_sarah_to_michael.md`, Sarah says Acme is "asking for 15% off list… and I think their agreement covers it." The knowledge base rejected that claim, and the master supply agreement isn't what allows the 15%. The authority is the separate exception EXC-ACME-NS500-15. Any quote or approval request should cite the exception ID, not the agreement.

**Background:** Acme is a Strategic/Enterprise account in Milwaukee, owned by Sarah Chen. As of 2026-09-23 it wanted to move ahead with the NS-500 and has a third-plant NS-500 rollout planned.
