## Question (verbatim)

> Could Sarah Chen have approved a 15% NS-500 discount for Acme Manufacturing on 2025-09-23?

## Answer

**No, it doesn't look like it.** The 15% discount itself was allowed for Acme on that date, but approving it was above Sarah Chen's authority.

**Why the 15% was allowed for Acme:**
- Exception **EXC-ACME-NS500-15** covers Acme and the NS-500 Industrial Controller. It sets a maximum discount of 15% of list price, runs from 2025-04-01 to 2028-03-31, and so was in force on 2025-09-23. It replaced the earlier EXC-ACME-NS500-10. (Acme's earlier 10% term ran from 2023-04-01 to 2025-03-31.)
- Sarah *prepared* the exception, and David Morgan (Chief Revenue Officer) approved it on 2025-03-20.
- The exception explicitly **does not grant approval authority.** It only says Acme qualifies for the discount; someone with the right authority still has to approve each deal.

**Why Sarah couldn't approve it:**
- Her title is Enterprise Account Executive, and she is Acme's account owner. She reports to Michael Torres, the VP Sales.
- The pricing policy in force on 2025-09-23 was **Northstar Pricing Policy 2025**, which ran from 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31. It lets Enterprise Account Executives approve discounts up to a limit, and anything above that needs VP Sales approval. It also requires recorded approval evidence.
- The specific limits I can find are **≤10% for Enterprise Account Executives** and **>10% up to ≤20% for VP Sales**. Under those limits, a 15% discount needed Michael Torres, not Sarah.

**One gap:** the ≤10% and >10% up to 20% figures come from a document tied to the 2026 policy, and the knowledge base doesn't record the 2025 policy's exact percentages. I'm assuming the limits were the same in 2025; if you need certainty, check the 2025 policy text directly.

**Possible date mix-up:** the request I found from Acme (moving ahead with the NS-500 controller and asking for 15% off list) is dated **2026-09-23**, not 2025. If that's the deal you mean, the 2026 policy applies, where the same ≤10% limit is clearly recorded. The answer is still no: Sarah would need VP Sales sign-off.
